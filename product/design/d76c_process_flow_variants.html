The process flow, four iterations

E is chosen: a layout per table — a process flow for Establishing a PGS group and SAOSO organic certification, a spreadsheet for the seals comparison. This page is only about the process shape. C was the first attempt; here it is with its narrow columns widened, its fee put back where the paper puts it, its invented titles marked, and the SAOSO branch shown. Pick the one that reads best.

Companion to d76b_reader_table_examples.html Real Step 1/2/3 text Self-contained — no network

How to read this

Four shapes for the same three steps. Each is the real reviewed text, so the difference is the layout. Part 1 is what C got right and wrong; Parts 2–5 are the iterations; Part 6 is the SAOSO branch, which is the test the establishing table cannot give; Part 7 recommends.

One thing to decide while you read: the short step titles (Get together, Agree and establish, Visit and certify) are reviewer-added — the paper prints only Step 1, Step 2, Step 3. They are useful for scanning, but they are data a person must write and defend. Each example works without them; the titles are shown so you can judge whether they earn their place.

Part 1 — What C got right, and what it got wrong

C’s choiceVerdictWhy
A numbered step, a connector, a cardKeepThe sequence is the content; the number and the line are what make it a flow rather than a grid.
The four other columns become facets of the stepKeepResources, seals and fee are properties of a step, not independent variables.
Three equal columns at the 52.5 rem paneChangeEach card is ~16 rem (~30 characters), so the 400-character Step 2 body becomes a 14-line narrow column. The pane has width; C spends it on three gutters.

Step 2 · Agree and establish p. 4

The PGS group agrees on operational procedures and their Basic Production Principles, based on an internationally accepted standard, against which farmers will be assessed.

The group completes a self-assessment form (like a status report), shares the required information with PGS SA and pays an annual membership fee.

Once formally established and paid-up members of PGS SA, groups will be entitled to make use of the “member of PGS SA” logo. Groups may also create their own local logo and use the “member of PGS SA” logo on their communication and certificates.

Groups that do not wish to design their own logo may make use of a PGS SA logo customized to the name of their PGS group.

Step 3 · Visit and certify p. 5

The group conducts farm visits and on approval grants certificates to its farmer members in the relevant category. Farmers may label their products accordingly. The use of the two seals is detailed alongside.

Resources
  • Farm visit assessment form
  • Farm visit checklist
  • Farm visit certificate granted to approved farms, which lists all the products and displays the relevant seal
Use of seals

These two seals were developed by PGS SA in consultation with PGS groups. Groups may also create their own seals. PGS groups are custodians of the manner in which the seals are used. Where exceptions to the organic standards are granted, a copy of the farm visit assessment report must be submitted to PGS SA.

PGS SA is not responsible for monitoring the use of these seals.

two seals (picture not stored)
Fee — certification (Step 3)

Recommended annual fee for the use of stickers (paid by individual farmers): R350, divided between PGS SA (30%) and PGS groups (70%). Printing of seals is at own cost.

What it fixes: the step body gets the pane’s width (~90 characters per line, not 30), the fee sits where the merged cell sits, and the phone shape is already vertical — no re-layout. What it costs: it is tall (about 1.5 screens on a desktop, 2.5 on a phone), and comparing Step 1’s resources with Step 3’s is still a scroll.

For

  • Reads like the document: a step, its facets, the next step.
  • The fee is in reading order, not in a band after the last step.
  • Works on the phone with no second layout.
  • The rail is one CSS pseudo-element.

Against

  • Tall; three steps is a long scroll.
  • Lateral comparison is still weak.
  • The connector is less visible than in C’s horizontal cards.

Part 6 — The test C cannot pass: SAOSO’s branch

SAOSO is Step 1 → Step 2 → or the direct-certification route. If the process shape cannot show a fork, it is not the process shape.

C4

C1 with a fork

The same timeline; the third route branches after Step 2 instead of continuing it.

1

Step 1 p. 7

For a minimum of 18 months, the PGS will be operating as per its agreed procedures and approach PGS SA for support. Farmers are assessed during farm visits and the PGS coordinator keeps a record of farm visits. Groups that have been compliant PGS SA members for this period can apply to SAOSO and PGS SA for approval to use the SAOSO “Organic PGS Certified” logo.

Resources
  • SAOSO application form
  • Appendix 9 of the SAOSO Standard for Organic Production and Processing
  • PGS SA Assessment of Group documents
Fee

Assessments by experienced PGS practitioners; fee determined on a case-by-case basis.

2

Step 2 p. 7

If approved by PGS SA the group is entitled to determine which farmers qualify to use the SAOSO “Organic PGS Certified” logo. Qualifying farmers sign a licensing agreement with SAOSO; the logo can be used for PGS-to-PGS trade.

Resources
  • List of eligible farmers in the group
  • SAOSO licensing agreement
  • Downloadable stickers
Fee

Licensing agreement with SAOSO: R500 (farmer members) / R2 500 (retail members). Printing at the farmer’s cost.

or, for farmers who cannot join a PGS group

Direct third-party certification p. 8

Farmers who are producing organically but are not able to join or establish a PGS group (geographic isolation, time constraints) can apply to SAOSO to use the SAOSO “Organic Certified” logo. This route excludes the social, knowledge-sharing and participatory approach of PGS groups and may not be cost effective for smallholder farmers.

Resources
  • Certification body application form
  • Licensing agreement
Fee

Quote from the Accredited Certifying Body; annual audit and licensing agreement on payment.

What it shows: the timeline survives the branch with one dashed connector and one label. C3’s three cards would need a fourth card that reads as a fourth step, which is wrong; C2’s tabs would hide the alternative. This is the strongest argument for C1 as the process shape.

For

  • A fork is legible: dashed line, “or”, an indented card.
  • The same component serves both tables.
  • No new data: the branch row is just another row.

Against

  • The direct route’s card is narrower than the steps (indented).
  • On a phone the dash and the indent need care.

recommend  C1 — the vertical timeline, with C4’s fork

A number-and-rail timeline; each step is a full-width block with its body, then its facets in a two-column grid; the merged Fee cell is a band between Step 2 and Step 3 (where the paper’s cell starts), and the Step 3 fee follows Step 3. The SAOSO table uses the same shape with a dashed fork for the direct route. All text is 13.5 px, the step label is bold, and the short titles are either kept as reviewed data or dropped — the layout works either way.

  • It is the only shape that gives the step body the pane. C’s narrow columns were the real cost; C1 spends the width on the words, which is what a document reader is here for.
  • The fee is where the paper puts it. The merged cell belongs to Steps 1–2, so the band sits after Step 2, not after Step 3. No amount is lifted out of its qualifying sentence (D72.2).
  • The fork is the proof. SAOSO needs it; C1 carries it with one dashed line. C2 cannot, and C3 has to add a fourth card.
  • It is the cheapest of the good shapes. No JS, one pseudo-element for the rail, and the phone is the same layout at a narrower measure.

What would change my mind: if the UAT shows the scroll is too long (open the page on a phone and count the screens), then the wizard (C2) is the compactness answer — but it must solve the fee-out-of-context problem and the citation-opens-a-closed-tab problem first. If the product owner wants the three steps visible side by side, C3 is the refinement of C; keep the fee ordering fix and accept the narrow columns.
