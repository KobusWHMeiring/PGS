Where is the money for an open-source PGS platform — and who signs?

A sweep of the funder landscape against two applicant shapes: you as an individual, and NU HORUS NPO as the group. The question this page exists to answer is which two routes deserve the next 90 days.

How to read this

Every claim carries one of three tags.

  • checked — I ran the command or fetched the page, and the quote is reproduced here. You can re-run it.
  • judgement — my reading of a call's text, or of how an assessor will see us. Arguable, and said so where it matters.
  • unverified — plausible, widely repeated, but not tested this session. Do not budget against it.

F1 asked whether one fund was worth a fortnight. This page asks a different question: across the whole landscape, where does this project's shape actually clear the gates, and what is the cheapest sequence that gets money moving?

1.4 Two facts that reshape the whole search

GIZ already pays for PGS knowledge products in Southern Africa. Page 9 of the certification-landscape PDF: the document “was funded by the South African Organic Sector Organisation (SAOSO), PGS South Africa (PGS SA) and the Knowledge Hub for Organic Agriculture in Southern Africa (KHSA) as part of the global project Knowledge Centre for Organic Agriculture in Africa (KCOA), implemented by the Deutsche Gesellschaft für Internationale Zusammenarbeit (GIZ) GmbH… on behalf of the German Federal Ministry for Economic Cooperation and Development (BMZ).”

That is not a call you can enter. It is a programme that has already bought the groundwork, with a South African partner chain — and it is the highest-probability money on this page.

The money that matters does not publish calls. I fetched 38 non-empty pages across roughly 32 organisations. Two publish a live dated deadline (NLnet, 3 Jan 2027; IDRC, 30 Nov 2026). One more publishes only a closed one. Foundation sites describe mission, not application routes.

A call-poller — including Tenderly — can only ever see that minority. The rest is a map and a conversation.

Part 2

The shape of the problem

F1's central insight still holds and it governs everything below: eligibility precedes merit. A proposal is screened on who is asking and what is being funded before anyone reads the idea. Three gates, in order, and the first one to fail ends the process:

GateThe questionWhere this project stands
1. Who signs Are you a person, an NPO, an EU consortium, a company? Two shapes available. The NPO is the broader key; the person is the narrower, faster key.
2. What is funded Code? Field operations? A person's time? A programme delivered with partners? This is the mismatch. The code is fundable as a commons; the group's real need is operations and rollout. They are different pots.
3. Relevance Does it sit on their axis — internet infrastructure, agroecology, AI for good, African agriculture? Genuinely split: the engine is infrastructure-shaped; the assistant is AI-for-good; the group is agroecology. No single funder likes all three.

That mismatch is the whole difficulty. Plot the landscape on two axes and the funders scatter:

Funds the code / commonsFunds operations / fieldFunds the person
You sign NLnet open call; Sovereign Tech Fellowship — (nothing pays a solo operator to run a farmer group's system) Ashoka, Echoing Green, MIT Solve
NPO signs NLnet (NPO eligible); tech-for-good foundations; DPGA as a credential GIZ/BMZ via KCOA·KHSA·SAOSO·PGS SA; WC Dept of Agriculture; agroecology funds — (the NPO cannot take a personal fellowship)

Read the bottom-left cell carefully: it is the only cell where the group's actual need — money to run and roll out a system — has a plausible payer. Everything else buys a different project.

Part 4

The three routes worth arguing about

Each gets its strongest case first, then its weaknesses. A route with no weaknesses below is one I have not understood.

A  The programme route

GIZ/BMZ → KCOA → KHSA → SAOSO → PGS SA → the NPO

Strongest case

They have already paid for the intellectual groundwork and the relationship chain is South African. A digital PGS tool is exactly what a knowledge programme funds next, and the NPO is the right shape to receive it. No European partner, no licence gate, no AI clause.

Against
  • There is no call to enter. It is a conversation, and conversations have no deadline to force a decision.
  • It is procurement-shaped: you may become a sub-contractor delivering someone else's workplan.
  • A programme usually wants the deliverable to serve its countries, not one Garden Route group.
  • It may expect the code to be handed over, or to stay closed — directly against the commons idea.

B  The FOSS route

NLnet open call · 3 Jan 2027 · €5–50k

Strongest case

Individuals and NPOs are both explicitly eligible. The deadline is fixed and knowable. The money buys technical work, which is the part already proven here. F1's O2 milestones were written for exactly this shape and remain reusable.

Against
  • “Our grants are primarily for technical development” — a field-rollout budget will be cut.
  • Their generative-AI policy means the chatbot cannot be the headline, and must be disclosed.
  • The engine must read as a building block. “A certification app for one farmer group” is not one.
  • It funds work that gets published — not the group's operations, now or later.

C  The credibility route

LICENSE → CI → DPGA → IFOAM/PGS → foundations

Strongest case

Costs about a week and changes the question every later funder asks. A licensed, CI-verified, DPG-recognised PGS engine is a different object from an unlicensed private repo — and the agroecology and tech-for-good foundations in stratum 3 and 5 have no call you can enter, so a credential is the only door.

Against
  • It produces no cash by itself. It is a precondition wearing the costume of a plan.
  • DPG status requires open licencing of the software; the corpus still cannot be published, so the “entirety” question returns in every form.
  • It delays the only application with a real deadline (B) if done first and badly.

D  The person route

Sovereign Tech Fellowship · Ashoka · Echoing Green

Strongest case

Fellowships fund a person to keep doing what they are already doing, which is the truest description of this project. No entity, no licence, no consortium. It buys the maintainer's time — the thing no grant in stratum 1 will pay for.

Against
  • They buy a trajectory and a personal story, not a repository. This repo's story is told in commits.
  • Cohorts and cycles are unpredictable; nothing here has a date you can plan to.
  • It does not fund the group, and the group is the thing with members and a bank account.

Where these four disagree is the real finding. B pays for the engine; A pays for the group's ability to use it; D pays for the person who maintains it; C is the only one that makes the other three credible. None of them funds “the platform” as a whole — which is the thing F1 established is unfundable as a single ask.

Part 5

What the pattern says

Four principles, drawn from the pages actually read, including the ones that cut against the recommendation.

PrincipleWhat it saysDoes it hold here?
Fund maintenance, not features Infrastructure funders pay for the unglamorous: packaging, docs, audits, CI. Yes — and it is exactly the work this repo has skipped (no CI, no licence, no install path).
Fund a named person Fellowships and many small grants evaluate a human, not a plan. Partly — the strongest asset here is a working system built by one person, which is the fellowship story.
Fund adoption, not code The question every second-round reviewer asks is “who else uses it?” This is the weakest point in every route. There is one adopter, and it is the group the code was built for. A second PGS group's one-line expression of interest is worth more than any paragraph of architecture.
Openness is a gate, not a virtue “Must be published under a recognised open source license in its entirety.” Currently fails. One hour fixes it for the code; the corpus needs a different answer (Part 1.3).

Where the sources disagree. NLnet says the best route for an unsure applicant is the open call, while its own EU funds would reject this applicant outright on geography. Sovereign Tech says it invests “globally” while framing every benefit in terms of German and European competitiveness. Both are true; the difference is who is reading. That is why the licence plus a second adopter outrank any single application.

Do not pursue

  • DGMT — states it is not a grant-maker.

  • EU consortium funds (Horizon, IKI) and the EU-backed NGI Zero funds — they need a European partner taking the majority of the money.

  • Any large US foundation until a first grant exists. Wrong ticket size, wrong stage.

What would change my mind

  • SAOSO says a KCOA follow-on is already scoped, with a budget and a delivery date — then A becomes the whole plan, and B is abandoned for this cycle.
  • A second PGS group says yes in writing — then B's weakest row strengthens enough to raise the ask toward €50k and pull attestations forward.
  • NLnet says AI-adjacent work is out of scope for the open call — then submit the engine with the retrieval layer described purely as infrastructure, or wait for the March deadline and de-risk first.
  • A university partner appears — NRF opens, and a research framing of participatory certification becomes available.
  • NU HORUS NPO turns out to lack PBO / s18A status — several foundations drop out, and the fiscal-host route becomes the practical answer.

What F1 got wrong, or could not know

  • The European dimension is not an NLnet property. It attaches to the EU-funded NGI Zero funds. The open call says “In principle anyone can apply: individuals…”. A South African applicant has a route F1 concluded did not exist.
  • Shuttleworth is not a live option — F1 did not consider it, and it would have been the obvious first suggestion.
  • The corpus-rights question is answered (Part 1.3): no rights are granted in the SAOSO standard or the OPP, and the landscape document is explicitly non-commercial. “FOSS in its entirety” means the engine, with the corpus loaded by the operator.
