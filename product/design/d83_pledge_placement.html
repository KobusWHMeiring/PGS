Decision D83 · Outeniqua PGS · 2026-11-04

Where does the farmer pledge sit — and may an account carry any declaration?

Now that the self-check ends with an opt-in account, is the pledge signed at account creation, at the application, or on the pre-visit checklist?

A decision document for the product owner. Everything in Part 1 was read out of the sources named in the margin. No production code is proposed here.

0How to read this

This is not a proposal and not a mockup. It exists to get one decision made, and its job is to be wrong in public if it is wrong. Part 1 is an inventory of what the governing documents actually say, including one document that contradicts itself. Part 3 gives three options; each gets its strongest case before its weakness. Part 7 makes one recommendation and says what would change it.

The claim you asked about — "the can-I-apply process doesn't need to pledge" — is correct, and Part 1 shows the pledge's own words are what settle it.

1What is actually true

1.1 The answer to "when?", in the OPP's own words

On application every prospective farmer/producer member of the Outeniqua PGS will do the following: 1. Sign the farmer member pledge. OPP §I "Membership procedure", knowledge/corpus/opp.md:12–13 — read, not recalled

The OPP places the pledge first in the application, ahead of supplying information (3), familiarising with the Basic Production Principles and SAOSO (4), and familiarising with the group's own procedures (5). It is an application step, not a registration step and not a visit step.

1.2 The pledge text itself rules out signing it before applying

All the land for which I/we are applying for PGS endorsement has been free of prohibited pesticides, herbicides and fertilisers for at least 12 months before sowing or planting in the case of annual production; 12 months before grazing or harvest for pastures and meadows and/or 18 months before harvest for other perennials… OPP, Farmer Pledge, knowledge/corpus/opp.md:611–614

The declaration is about an application. A person who has not yet said what land, which categories and what turnover cannot truthfully sign a sentence that begins "all the land for which I am applying". This is a textual argument, not a design preference: the pledge presupposes the application step it would be moved ahead of.

2The shape of the problem

The difficulty is not "where does one signature go". It is that four consecutive stages each hold a different kind of obligation, and a blueprint that blurs them produces a system that asks for a declaration at the moment of least commitment.

StageWhat the farmer givesWhat the group may do with itReversible?
4 · am I readyanswers, optionallyaggregate totals, below a threshold never publishedyes — withdraw deletes both rows
5 · accountidentity + three farm fieldshold it and use it againyes — it is not an application
6–7 · apply + pledgedeclarations, including what is on the landassess, and hold the member to itno — it is evidence

The asymmetry that drives the decision: stages 4 and 5 are cheap to undo by design. Stage 7 is not — a pledge is the moment the group gains the right to remove a member and deny the use of the logo (the pledge says so, opp.md:604–605). Attaching that right to an action a farmer can abandon without consequence (creating an account) is the failure mode to avoid. The converse failure — a farmer who has done real work at stage 4 and 5 and must retype it — was the problem your account decision already solved.

3.1 Evidence for and against

A — Pledge stays in the application recommended

Strongest case for

  • It is what OPP §I says, in one sentence, with no interpretation required.
  • The pledge text cannot be signed truthfully before the application: it says "all the land for which I/we are applying".
  • It keeps the reversible/irreversible boundary where the system already draws it (§2 above): stages 4–5 undo cleanly, 7 does not.
  • It needs no new schema. Pledge hangs off Member, which the account already creates (_core.py, commit 243cedd).
  • Task A3 as planned already produces exactly this; nothing in the plan moves.

Weakest points

  • Two more screens between "I'm interested" and "I'm in" — the funnel is longer than it needs to be for a farmer who is already convinced.
  • An account that never applies is a person the group knows nothing about — no declaration, no categories. It is a lead, not a member, and the platform must say so.
  • It relies on the farmer reaching stage 6 at all; the account step does not bind them.

B — Pledge at account creation

Strongest case for

  • Shortest funnel: the moment of highest motivation is right after the result page says "these records are in place".
  • One declaration screen instead of two, so there is no drift between a light confirmation and the real pledge.
  • An abandoned account would still carry a signed pledge, which some groups would treat as a stronger statement of intent than an application alone.

Why it loses

  • The text does not permit it. "All the land for which I/we are applying" — at account creation nobody has said what they are applying for.
  • It makes the group the holder of signed pledges from people who never applied. The pledge's own clause about removal and loss of the logo (opp.md:604–605) would attach to a non-applicant.
  • It puts the land-history attestation before the categories, so the "annuals / pasture / other perennials" boxes are being ticked by someone who has not yet said which they produce.
  • A pledge is evidence. Evidence attached to a reversible action needs a withdrawal story the account step does not have, and §11 has just made a point of not linking SelfCheckReport to Member.

C — Two-tier (light confirmation, then the pledge)

Strongest case for

  • Keeps the pledge where it belongs while still giving the account step something to confirm, so the farmer feels the details are "theirs".
  • Matches how most services behave: a light terms acknowledgement, then the substantive declaration at the transaction.

Why it loses

  • It is the exact risk SELF_CHECK_PRD §7 already names: "two questionnaires that disagree". Two declaration-shaped screens are one edit away from contradicting each other, and there is no test that can tell you whether a "confirmation" is a declaration.
  • The light text has to be written, reviewed and consented — real cost for a screen whose only job is reassurance.
  • The account step already is a confirmation: typing your farm name and pressing save is the confirmation.

4.1 The constraint this page found in Task A3

A3's conflict rule is written against the wrong reference. The plan says land_history_conflict is set "when a declared period disagrees with OPP §V (annuals 18, pasture 12, other perennials 12)". But the pledge a farmer signs, and SAOSO 4.2.3, both say 12 / 12 / 18. So a farmer who declares exactly what the pledge prints would be flagged as conflicted, every time.

Two ways to fix it, and this page recommends the second:

  • Compare against both and flag a three-way disagreement — more code, and it still treats a document-level contradiction as a per-farmer fault.
  • Make the conflict a constant, not a computation. The contradiction is between two paragraphs of the OPP; no farmer's answer creates it. D31 is recorded as a module-level fact naming both readings, and the pledge row stores only what was declared. A per-row boolean that is always true or always false is not information (the same reasoning S4 applied to the extraction mode in D80).

6Recommendation

A — the pledge stays in the application, and the account carries no declaration

Sign it at stage 7, after the account, as OPP §I says and as the plan already has it. The account step collects the three fields you decided on today and nothing else. The result page's call to action stays "Create an account", never "Join".

What it changes: three things to write down, no code beyond what A3 already plans — (1) the account CTA wording, so a farmer does not read account creation as joining; (2) the plan's land_history_conflict rule, per §4.1; (3) one line in the blueprint above as the reference for every later "where does X happen" question.

What would change my mind: if the group decides the pledge is an entry declaration rather than an application declaration — but then OPP §I and the pledge's own wording have to be amended first, because the platform should not be the place that resolves it. Or: if data later shows a large cohort of accounts that never apply, and the group wants those people bound by something — that would reopen the question with evidence rather than argument.
