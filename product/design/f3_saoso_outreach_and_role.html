Two documents to approve: the SAOSO email, and the role that it leads to

The email is 250 words and asks for nothing but twenty minutes. The role description is what a programme would actually fund — because "build me a platform" is not a thing donors buy, and "pay one person to run it" is.

Why these two, in this order

SAOSO is a current grantee of the Agroecology Fund, with a live project that names PGS in its title and runs to 30 June 2027. The fund has no open call — nominators are Advisors, Funders and Long-term Partners — so the only way in is through an existing relationship. That is what the email opens.

The role description exists because of what you told me: you want build time and a salary, and with those you would run the group's operations yourself. No funder pays an open-ended salary. Programmes do fund posts. So the second document reframes the ask into the only shape that money recognises.

Document 1

The email

Deliberately short, specific, and it invites a no. Two things it does not do: it does not describe the code, and it does not ask for money.

Email — first contact

Subject: Digital PGS — something you may already be funding
Dear [name], We spoke some time ago about the Outeniqua group's move off paper. That system is now live at organicguarantee.africa: member records, the governing documents served directly instead of through shared folders, and a way to ask the SAOSO standard a question and get the passage it came from. I am writing because of your Agroecology Fund project — "Scaling Agroecology for Justice: Strengthening Community-led Food Systems and Biodiversity through Participatory Guarantee System (PGS), Advocacy and Regional Collaboration", which I see runs to June 2027. Three questions: 1. Is there a digital workstream inside that project, or room for one in a renewal? 2. Would a shared engine be useful to other PGS groups — that is, is this Outeniqua's problem or the network's? 3. If it is the network's, who would host the person who builds and runs it? I am not asking for a proposal, and I would rather be told plainly if this is not useful to you than take up your time. Twenty minutes, in person or on a call, is all I am after — I would show you what exists and then listen. Kind regards, Kobus Meiring Outeniqua PGS [phone] · organicguarantee.africa

Why this works. It leads with what exists rather than what is planned, quotes their own project title verbatim (which proves you read it), names the network question instead of asking for money, and explicitly offers them an easy refusal. A programme officer can reply to this in one line, which is the whole point.

Two things to fill in before sending. [name] — your existing contact, not the general address. And check whether the project has already ended for your counterpart before you cite it: the page lists Ongoing · 01/07/25 – 30/06/27 checked 2026-11-03, but a project being "ongoing" on a website and being staffed are not the same fact.

Document 2

The role description

One page. Written to be lifted into a programme document or a job advert without editing, which is why it names deliverables instead of features.

PGS Digital Systems Coordinator

PGS DIGITAL SYSTEMS COORDINATOR 1. Purpose One person responsible for the digital system that carries a participatory guarantee system's assessment record and public guarantee — for the network, not for a single farm. 2. Why the role exists A PGS group's integrity rests on its records: who was visited, by whom, against what standard, with what evidence, and what the committee decided. Today those records are paper. Paper does not lose data, but it cannot answer a consumer, cannot be searched, and cannot be audited without a filing cabinet. Software that nobody maintains reverts to paper within a season. The common failure of a donor-funded tool is that it is handed over without a person, and then abandoned. This role exists to be that person. 3. Responsibilities - Maintain and extend the platform: the assessment lifecycle from application to certificate, and the public surface that shows what is currently true. - Take the farm visit into the field: a two-hour walkabout completed on a phone with no signal, reconciled on return, with no retyping by the administrator. - Keep the standard current: ingest each revision, re-link the citations, and prove the reader still matches the printed page. - Train the assessors, the committee and the administrator; write the operator documentation. - Support a second group adopting the same engine with a different standard — the step that turns one group's tool into shared public infrastructure. 4. Year-one deliverables 1. A complete farm visit, end to end, offline on a phone, on the server before the assessor is back in reception. 2. A public verification surface: any current certification outcome resolvable by a code, and nothing published that is not currently true. 3. A second PGS group running its own standard on the same engine with no code changes. 4. An open licence, an install path and operator docs — so the system survives the person. 5. Requirements - Python and Django, with enough database confidence to own a data model. - Able to work with farmers and a committee, not only with developers. - Patience for a standard that arrives as a PDF, and rigour about quoting it exactly. - Written English clear enough to be the group's public voice. 6. Cost The cost of one mid-level technologist employed in South Africa for a year, budgeted as a salary line rather than a consultancy fee. Indicative range, to be confirmed against the host organisation's own scales: R480 000 - R720 000 total cost of employment per annum, including equipment and data. This is a planning figure, not a quote. 7. Hosting For a programme funder this matters more than the amount: the post should sit where the mandate is national. Options, in order of durability: - SAOSO, as the standards owner, with the post serving all member groups. - PGS South Africa, as the PGS body. - Outeniqua's own NPO, seconded to the national body, as an interim arrangement. 8. What this role is not Not a consultancy deliverable, not a fixed-term build, and not a volunteer. The failure mode this role is designed to prevent is a system that is delivered and then unowned.

The load-bearing sentence is §7. A funder will not pay for one group's tool. The same role described as a national capability — serving every PGS group that adopts the standard — is a different and much better-shaped proposition. That is also why deliverable 3 exists: it is the proof that the post is national, not local.

Notes for the next session

Before you send

The five things not to say

The same discipline the public site is held to applies to a fundraising email — a claim that cannot be backed is the fastest way to lose a programme officer's trust.

  • Do not lead with "AI chatbot." Lead with the standard being answerable and citations being checkable. The mechanism is unremarkable; the guarantee is not. Several FOSS funds also refuse AI-led projects outright.
  • Do not say the members are certified. Membership is not certification, and the distinction is the whole integrity of a PGS.
  • Do not claim adoption you do not have. One group uses this. That is the honest sentence, and "one group, and here is what it would take to serve ten" is a better pitch than an inflated number.
  • Do not say "sustainable" without naming the revenue. If the answer is "grant, then another grant", say that instead — programmes respect a plan more than an adjective.
  • Do not confuse free with free. The licence costs nothing; running the system costs a person. Asking for the person is the credible ask.
