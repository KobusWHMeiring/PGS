D46 · Corpus column extraction · MVP 0 · 2026-10-20

Does the chat work better now?

Yes — for the refusal this work was built to fix. Not yet for every refusal. The corpus can now be quoted from. If the model quotes the standard exactly and names a real locator, the audit accepts it. What remains is the model’s citation habits, retrieval ranking, and the empty vector arm — all named below, none hidden.

This page is the flow as it runs today: where a question enters, where the text is stored, where the citation check sits, and the five places the flow has failed. Each carries a status label: Fixed, Open, Verified once (D47), or By design.

The short answer

What changed, and what did not

The manure question — “Do I need to know where horse manure comes from before I can use it?” — was refused twice on 2026-10-19. The corpus answered it, and the model kept producing the right answer with a quote. The quote could not be checked because the page was two-column: pdftotext -layout put both columns on one visual line, so a sentence that spans the gutter did not exist as a contiguous run in any stored chunk. The audit was right; the data was wrong.

As of 2026-10-20 the page is de-columned at ingest. The BPP sentence and the SAOSO Appendix 2 condition are each contiguous inside one stored chunk, the verbatim sentence is accepted by audit_citations, and the model’s recorded paraphrase is still refused. A live turn is refused today only if the model supplies a citation that cannot be checked, or retrieval does not return the passage.

29of 33 detected pages re-ordered at ingest
4documented exclusions, with reasons
340chunks, all carrying a 768-dim vector
536tests green; architecture contract kept

What “better” does not mean. It does not mean every question now answers. The audit still refuses a paraphrase, an elided quote, an invented locator, or a quote from a chunk retrieval never returned. Those refusals are the feature, not a regression — but the user sees the same warm refusal either way, so the operator must read the reason before calling it a corpus gap.

Part 3 · The five ways a question gets refused

One fixed, three model/retrieval, one by design

#MechanismWhat the user seesStatusWhere the fix belongs
F1 Corpus interleaving. A two-column page stored as alternating columns; a sentence spanning the gutter is not a substring of any chunk. A refusal that says the quote is not in the cited chunk — even when the model’s answer and quote are correct. Fixed knowledge/columns.py, wired at ingest. Includes the measured BPP PDF 4 band override (40, 42), which makes the raw-manure 60-day rule contiguous. Proven by stored-chunk + audit tests.
F2 Paraphrase or elision. The model rewrites the standard, or joins two passages with .... The quote cannot be found on paper. A refusal naming “the quote is not in the cited chunk”. By design Verified once (D47) The audit stays strict. D47 makes the prompt require character-for-character quotes and forbid an ellipsis; verified on one live turn 2026-10-20.
F3 Invented sub_ref. The model returns a heading or label where a stored sub-ref is expected. A refusal naming the sub_ref as not existing in the chunk. Verified once (D47) D47: SYSTEM_PROMPT and build_prompt() omit sub_ref unless the passage lists it; verified on one live turn 2026-10-20.
F4 Retrieval ranking. A rare word wins over the passage that answers the question; “horse” ranks Appendix 6b’s housing table first. A refusal, or a grounded answer citing the wrong-but-topical passage. Open The rare-word/vector collision is fixed (rare-term arm lexical, vector once on the OR query); Appendix 6b still ranks first for “horse”, so the ranking defect itself remains.
F5 No embeddings. 0 of 340 chunks carried a vector; retrieval was lexical only. Worse recall on questions whose words do not appear verbatim in the standard. Fixed (D48) Done 2026-10-20: gemini-embedding-001 @ 768, forced re-ingest, 340/340 embedded; lexical_only=False. A question spends one query embedding.

F1 was the only one this work owned. The PRD’s non-goals are explicit: no loosening of the audit, no retrieval ranking, no re-embedding, no prompt changes. A page that claims all five are fixed would be lying about three of them.

Part 5 · What to do when it refuses

Read the reason before calling it a corpus gap

The user-visible refusal is always the same warm string (D17). The escalation records a reason, and the audit’s problems name the mechanism. Use this table, not intuition.

Audit problem / symptomMost likely causeAction
“the quote is not in the cited chunk”, and the quote’s wording differs from the standard F2 paraphrase (or an elision with ...) Model/prompt follow-up. Do not edit the corpus to match a paraphrase.
“sub_ref … does not exist in the chunk” F3 invented sub-ref Model/prompt follow-up. The audit is correct.
“not present in the retrieved set” F4 retrieval miss/ranking Check the retrieved chunk list; file under the retrieval PRD.
Every citation names a two-column document, and the quoted words cannot be found on the printed page F1 corpus interleaving — should now be historical Check IngestReport.layout_pages for that document; re-ingest if it is empty.
Grounded answer, citation resolves Working as intended Still do UAT: can you find the quote on paper?

Part 6 · The one decision left

Where should the model-side refusals be fixed? D47 · Decided A

F1 is closed. F2 and F3 remain, and both are citation-contract problems. The PRD for the corpus fix deliberately does not touch prompts or the audit, so this was a real choice, not a cleanup.

Decided 2026-10-20: option A. Recorded as D47 in PGS_PLATFORM_SPEC.md §18.3 and implemented in SYSTEM_PROMPT. The audit was not changed. The live manure turn was re-run under d47-1 (three verbatim Appendix 2 citations), then D48 embedded the corpus and moved the version to d47-2 (two verbatim Appendix 2 citations). **Two further iterations followed:** the informal “can i use horse manure?” still produced stitched quotes — first with an ellipsis, then across two numbered passages — so d47-3 and d47-4 made the one-contiguous-excerpt rule explicit, in the prompt and in the schema. The same question then answered with six verbatim citations. The audit still refuses a paraphrase by design.

A — Tighten the prompt/citation contract

Decided
  • For: fixes the actual cause; the audit stays strict; no corpus or audit change; testable with the existing fake-model harness.
  • Against: a prompt is a request, not a guarantee; a future model may still misbehave, and the refusal is the safety net.

B — Repair the model’s citations before the audit

Rejected
  • For: fewer refusals with no corpus work; the answer text is unchanged.
  • Against: silently changes what the model said; a quote with an elision is still not a quote the reader can find; dropping a sub-ref loses lookup precision. This is the “accept the checkable part” rule the audit exists to refuse.

C — Leave it; refusals escalate to a human

Rejected
  • For: zero risk of weakening the audit; the refusal is honest and the human sees the question.
  • Against: the product owner’s exact question still refuses when the model misbehaves, and the queue grows with questions the corpus can answer.

What would change my mind: evidence that the model cannot be steered by the contract — repeated live runs across two prompt revisions still inventing sub-refs or ellipses. Then a narrowly-scoped repair (B) for the sub_ref only, never for the quote, would be the lesser evil.
