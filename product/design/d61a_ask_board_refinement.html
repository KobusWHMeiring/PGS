Ask first. The board is underneath.

The question field is the primary thing on the page, and “questions we have answered” sits below it as the library you scroll into. The consent checkbox is the one new control: a visitor decides whether their question joins the public database.

Why the input is first

The board is the library, but the page is called ask. A visitor with a question in their head should not have to scroll past six cards to find the box. The library is the payoff, not the entry point.

Why the box is opt-in

D59 says we may keep a transcript, not that we may publish it, and a question is free text from a public form. Unticked is the honest default: silence is not consent.

What the promise costs

The label says “an administrator reads it before it appears”, so the card lands as awaiting review, not live. A one-step publish would be simpler and would put unread visitor text on a public page.

Do I need to know where my horse manure comes from?

Yes — the source decides the rule. Extensive livestock farming is fine; intensive or conventional needs permission and composting; factory-farming origin is forbidden.

Can I use cardboard from my neighbour?

The standard does not name cardboard. It governs it as a biodegradable by-product: free of significant contaminants, or composted first.

What is the yearly cost to get certified, including the farm visit?

Four things, and you need all four: R100 membership, the turnover-band assessment fee, per-visit catering, and the seal licence if you sell with it.

If my ducks are not fed organic food and I compost their bedding, will I still pass?

Conditional: the bedding material and the compost both have to satisfy Appendix 2, and the feed has to meet S5.5 — 5.5.1 requires that animals are fed organic feed.

Can I use straw mulch, and does it have to be untreated?

Straw is listed with crop residues and plant materials, with no condition attached in that row. The “untreated” condition belongs to wood, bark and sawdust — not to straw.

What the checkbox does

Four states, two columns

The control only makes sense if it behaves predictably in all four states. This is the whole contract, and it is what a test would pin.

StateBox tickedBox not ticked
Grounded answer The answer shows under the input and a card is added to the board, marked awaiting review until an administrator has read it. The answer shows under the input. Nothing is published; the transcript is kept privately (D59).
Refusal A Waiting card is added — the gap becomes visible, and other visitors stop asking the same unanswerable question. An EscalatedQuery is created for the administrator. The visitor sees the refusal only.
Already on the board The existing card's “asked N times” goes up. No duplicate is added. Nothing changes on the board; the visitor still gets a fresh answer.
The administrator rejects it The card is removed. The answer already shown is unaffected — it never depended on the card. Not applicable.

The open question this raises. If a visitor ticks the box and the question is a duplicate, should they be told? Telling them is honest and stops the count inflating — but it also reads as “your question doesn’t matter”. The prototype does neither silently: it shows the existing card and still answers the question in full.
