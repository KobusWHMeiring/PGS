A PGS is defined by IFOAM–Organics International as a locally focused quality assurance system.

The PGS

Guided by six basic elements

A Participatory Guarantee System is not an inspection regime. It is a group of peers who hold each other to a shared standard. These six elements are what make it a PGS rather than a certificate bought from an agency.

01

Shared Vision

Members share a common goal based on a shared need, and collectively embrace the principles of PGS and organic agriculture.

02

Trust

An integrity-based approach. Trust and integrity form the ethical basis of the system.

03

Horizontality

A flat, equitable organisational structure, developed and managed by peers rather than a top-heavy management. All are responsible, all are accountable.

04

Transparency

The system is open to public, stakeholder and peer scrutiny at all levels.

05

Participatory

Members participate in developing and managing the process and procedures the group owns, controls and determines collectively.

06

Learning Process

Assessments and site visits focus on skills and knowledge exchange — traditional knowledge and innovation, soil, climate and market.

“Participatory Guarantee Systems are locally focused quality assurance systems. They certify producers based on active participation of stakeholders and are built on a foundation of trust, social networks and knowledge exchange.” IFOAM–Organics International · quoted in the SA Organic Certification Landscape

Membership procedure

How it works

Every prospective farmer and producer member follows the same path, in the same order. It is set out in the group's Principles, Policies and Procedures.

StepWhat happens
01ApplyApproach the group as a prospective farmer or producer member.
02PledgeSign the farmer member pledge. A recorded verbal pledge is accepted where reading is a barrier.
03ShareSupply the group with the information it needs to assess your operation.
04PrepareFamiliarise yourself with the Basic Production Principles and the SAOSO standard.
05VisitA peer assessment team visits the farm and works through the assessment.
06ReviewThe committee reviews the findings and records a decision.

Who is in the group

⚠ Membership is not certification

The people and farms listed here are members of the Outeniqua Natural and Organic PGS group. Membership of the group is not a certification, and does not by itself mean that a farm is certified organic. A certification decision is a separate judgement made by the group's certification committee against the SAOSO standard, and is recorded as such. Please do not read this list as a claim about any farm's organic status.
