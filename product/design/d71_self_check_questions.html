A front door that reproduced that would not be a front door. It would be the visit.

2. The FORM already separates the farmer's half from the assessor's

Every Question N is the white half — the farmer's own description. Every V# immediately after it is the grey half — the administrator's verdict, on the visit, against evidence.

This is why a preview cannot accidentally become a verdict: verdicts are respondent: ADMINISTRATOR and hang off a FarmVisit that does not exist yet (MVP1A_PRD §5.1). The form does the discipline for us.

3. “Not critical” is not a softening — it is already the rule in the code

Your note on B1 asked for “not critical that you have them”. That is not a copy decision, it is the specified behaviour of the visit:

A visit cannot be finalised with an unset `RecordCheck` or without a `SocialEquity` row. A `MISSING` record is a recorded fact, not a blocker — Homtini's missing sales records did not stop the visit; blank did.

MVP1A_PRD §5.8, D45

So Group B can say it plainly, and it is true: a missing record does not stop an assessment. What stops one is a question left blank. The front door now states the rule the server enforces, instead of a reassurance that might disagree with it.

Part 2

The shape of the problem, revised

Your notes converged on one thing I had underweighted: every group-B question reads as a demand until it says what happens if the answer is no. That is now the spine of the page.

Four constraints no question may cross

It asks a fact, never a status. No question implies the group has judged anything. The result screen says so in one fixed line.

It is coarse on purpose. Bands and short answer sets, not values. The application asks for exact hectares and exact figures; the front door asks roughly. A band is also what makes an aggregate safe to publish.

It asks nothing it cannot act on. Every question either changes the to-do list, changes the honest answer about timing or cost, or feeds the aggregate.

It never leaves a “no” hanging. New this revision. Each record, each practice and each consent says what happens next when the answer is no — because “no” was where every one of your notes landed.

Why a “Why we ask” line, and not a tooltip

You suggested tooltips on C3 and C4. I have used a native disclosure (<details>) instead, and put it on all twenty questions:

A tooltip needs JavaScript and a hover, and it is unreachable on a phone and invisible to print. A disclosure works with the keyboard, on a touch screen, with no JS, and it prints open. It also puts the clause on the page in text, where a reviewer can check it against the document — which a tooltip never does.

What a band costs you, and what a value would cost us

A2 and A5 are bands rather than figures for two reasons at once: a farmer is far more likely to answer “20–100 ha” than an exact hectare count on a first visit, and an exact hectare figure plus a category plus a turnover figure in a catchment of a few dozen farms is a farm you can name. The band is the privacy control and the completion control.

Part 4

What the result must never say

The result is the whole risk of this feature, so it is specified as a set of prohibitions rather than a layout.

NeverWhyInstead
“You are ready to apply” / “you qualify” / “you are eligible” A claim about a farm, on a public route, sourced to nobody. eligible is already used legitimately on /become-a-member/, so the guard has to be route-scoped. “Of the seven records a visit checks, your answers show four in place.”
A score, a percentage, a progress ring A number invites comparison and reads as an assessment result. Counts out of a known denominator, item by item.
The word certified, or any seal MVP 0 publishes no seal, active or otherwise; seals are MVP 2. “the visit decides”.
A single conversion-period number D31 — two governing documents disagree, and the conflict is unresolved. Both periods, the fact that they differ, and “plan for the longer”.
A fee band the farmer did not declare BUILD §III — declared, not computed. The platform stores the band the farmer chose; it never derives one from sales data. The band they ticked, with the OPP’s own figures beside it.

The one fixed line, on every result

“The farm visit decides. This is only what your answers show, and the visit happens after you apply.”

The second fixed line, added this revision

“A missing record does not stop an assessment. The visit records it, and you and the group agree what happens next.”

This is MVP1A_PRD §5.8 in a farmer’s words. It appears under Group B and again under the to-do list, because it is the sentence that turns “no” from a rejection into an instruction.

Part 6

What is still open

A. The one thing I could not answer for you

E2 — “if we wanted to apply to funding applications, what information would be valuable to capture?” That is a new purpose, and a purpose cannot be added by wording: the consent text names what is collected and what it is for, so a funding dataset needs its own consent, its own field list and its own retention answer. Some of what a funder asks for the page already collects in bands (category, hectares, turnover if A5 is kept, land history); employment numbers come from the visit’s social-equity section (SocialEquity), which is not on the front door.

The page therefore says what the census is for, in the FORM’s own words — “to profile small-scale organic farmers in South Africa for advocacy and support purposes” — and states plainly that funding applications are not covered. Proposing which fields a funder needs is a group decision, not a design one.

B. Two domain questions your notes surfaced that no document answers

1. Is there an unwritten freshness rule for soil and water tests? Neither SAOSO nor the assessment form sets a maximum age. Homtini’s test is several years old and the farm remains verified — which suggests the practice, but practice is not a rule. Until the group states one, the page says only “tell us when it was taken”.

2. Is “a mix” enough? For the front door, yes — it changes nothing and it is honest. For the visit, no: the FORM records the status of each seed lot, because that is the baseline the group measures improvement against. The Why we ask on C3 says exactly that, so a farmer who answers “a mix” learns what it will cost them later rather than being told it is fine.

C. A5 — the proposal that is not in your export

Cost is the first thing a farmer asks, and the page does not answer it — while /become-a-member/ already has a “which band are you in?” panel. Turnover is the only input the fee scale uses (OPP §2.3), so one band question would let the result print the actual figure.

It is also the most commercially sensitive question on the page, so: bands only, an explicit “rather not say”, and no fee figure if skipped. It is marked new — not in your export. Keep or drop it and the export updates.

D. What would change my mind

If, reading Part 3 on your own phone, you would not finish it — then it is three questions too long and Group B is what to cut, because A and C carry the aggregate value and D is the only question that answers “why don’t they join”.

If the fourth answer (“rather not say”) turns out to be most people’s answer to the records block, then the block is asking the wrong question at this moment and should move inside the application, after the farmer has decided to trust the group.
