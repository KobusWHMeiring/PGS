Fitting the landscape’s five-column tables into the reader

The reader’s text measure is 38 rem (≈85 characters), and the table’s own pane is narrower still: 52.5 rem once the tree takes 17 rem and the gap 2.5 rem. The landscape’s printed rows run to 169 characters of content, and the fee sits in the last column. How should the reader show the table so the fee is reachable — on a desktop and on a phone?

The correction that matters. §5.4 says the widest row is 323 characters and that at 160 characters it is “still ~2× too wide”. The 323 is the width of the page’s blank padding line (and of the right-aligned footer), not of the printed row. On every table page the row’s last printed character sits at 153–169 — about one column past the working column, not twice it. The fit problem is real, but it is one column’s worth, not two.

And the pane is not the page. The grid lives in the passages pane, 52.5 rem, because the tree takes 17 rem and the gap 2.5. A “breakout to 72 rem” would put the table under the sticky tree. The practical ceiling for option A is 52.5 rem — which at 14 px holds ~134 characters, so the fee starts just at the edge and finishes ~25–40 characters past it.

1.2  What the reader renders today

checked Today the reviewed-table path (structure_for) knows only the two-column appendix rows held in Python. For the landscape’s artefact tables, section_ref is None, so the reader falls back to the generic passage renderer: one block of the row’s cells joined by line breaks, at the 38 rem measure. That is the definition-list shape D72.3 rejected — the fee is a sentence buried in the middle of a column, not a column a reader can scan.

1.3  Why the fee is the test

The reader-reported question is “what does it cost to join a PGS group?”. The answer is the merged Fee cell: “Annual membership fee (paid by the PGS group to PGS SA): R500” and the once-off R1 000, in the fifth column, 127–149 characters from the left edge. Any option that cannot put that column within a glance at 1440 px has failed the one job the page was reported for.

Part 2 — The shape of the problem

Five columns, one of them a seal picture, one a 400-character prose cell. The constraint is not the total width; it is what has to stay legible per column while the fee stays reachable.

Three constraints pull in different directions:

  1. The reading measure is a measure of prose. 38 rem is right for a paragraph; it is wrong for a reference grid, where the eye scans a column, not a line. The reader already breaks it for the appendix caption (.reader__table-bar) — tables are the one place the measure is allowed to give way.
  2. A literal grid is tall, not wide. Once the cells wrap, the table’s width is set by the widest unbreakable token (a URL, a seal name), not by the 169-character printed line. At 14 px and 72 rem, five columns average ~14 rem each; the fee needs ~12 rem and the prose column ~22.
  3. Most readers are on a phone. 390 px is ~40 characters. No horizontal grid is readable there, sticky column or not; the phone needs the row restacked, with the column names attached to their values.

The asymmetry that decides it: on a desktop the problem is one breakout plus a safety net; on a phone it is a different layout, not a narrower one. Options that treat both viewports the same fail one of them.

Part 4 — What the evidence says

Verified, not argued:

  • The 323 figure is a blank padding line; the row content ends at 153–169 checked. The PRD’s “2×” is wrong; the gap is roughly one column.
  • At 72 rem and 14 px, option A’s grid fits two real rows with no scroll in the mock above checked in this page. The fee is the fifth column and is visible.
  • At 38 rem, options A (unbroken), B (before scrolling) and D all hide the fee checked.
  • At 390 px, only C has no horizontal gesture checked.
  • The phone is the majority case for a smallholder-facing page (the site’s own e2e suite tests at 390 px), so a desktop-only answer is a partial answer.

assumed The 38 rem ≈ 85 and 72 rem ≈ 160 character conversions are the CSS comment’s estimates at 16 px Mulish; the mock’s fit is observed, not measured by a script. A browser measurement would replace this assumption with a number.

Part 7 — Recommendation

recommend  A + B + C, with D as the multiplier

A real <table> that uses the whole passages pane (52.5 rem) rather than the 38 rem measure, with wrapped cells and a 14 px grid; a scroll region with a sticky first column for the last few characters of the fee column and for zoom; and the same markup stacked below 900 px, with each cell’s column name attached via data-label. Same HTML, CSS-only behaviour, no JavaScript.

  • It answers the question at every width. The fee is in view on a desktop (A), reachable with a short scroll and a pinned step label at 1200 px (B), and labelled rather than positional on a phone (C).
  • It keeps the product decision. The printed grid survives where there is room; the phone gets the same data, restacked, which is the only honest way to honour “the fee is reachable” at 390 px.
  • It is one template branch and one stylesheet. No new model field, no migration: the columns and legend come from the artefact, the rows from the chunks.

What it changes: knowledge/tables.py gains an artefact-table reader; core/services/_documents.py carries the grid on the Reader; document_reader.html renders the table; reader.css carries the three behaviours; DESIGN.md gains the component; tests pin the five column names, the fee row, and the prose outside the grid.

What would change my mind: if the 52.5 rem grid makes the fee cell wrap so far that the amount is not visible without a long scroll (verify in the mock at 840 px), then the breakout is not enough and the grid should be full-bleed below the tree instead. If the product owner prefers the reading measure to be inviolable, then C everywhere is the coherent answer, and we lose the grid on the desktop too.
