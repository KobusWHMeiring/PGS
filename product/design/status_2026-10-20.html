Part 3 — Up next

The delivery sequence, and where the arrow stops

Ordered by risk retired per unit of effort. Two facts drive it: the corpus is shared work — the assessment templates are derived from SAOSO clauses — and the public site is the surface the group needs before it will trust the platform with certification records.

1∥Seam contract. Cannot start.
1aIn-office visit flow. Ready; A–F ungated.
1bOffline walkabout. Where the MVP 1 claim attaches.
2Decision, certificate, registry, QR.

built available now blocked by a precondition not started Each node depends on the one before it; 0∥ is the only one that blocks two others.

Note the shape of the claim: 1a does not make field work paperless. On its own it means paper in the field and digital re-entry afterwards — more work than today. The MVP 1 claim therefore attaches to 1b, which is why the slice is two increments and one ship gate rather than two releases (D25).

The structural insight

All the irreversible risk sits in 1a — Assessment.template_schema freezes as evidentiary history at the first captured visit. All the unknowable risk sits in 1b — browser storage on real devices. Building the determinable half first is the entire point of the split, and it is why MVP 1a can start before the spike returns, while the seam contract cannot.

Refinement 1 · no build approved

Is this blueprint only for Outeniqua?

The measured finding is sharper than the claim it tests: of the eight source documents, seven are national — zero occurrences of "outeniqua" in the SAOSO standard, the FORM, the BPP, the landscape, the prep list or the three checklists. Only the OPP is group-owned (14 hits). The FORM is deliberately written group-neutral.

Three deployment shapes are laid out. Shape i, one cloned instance per branch, is recommended because it matches build_principles.md §IX verbatim. Shape ii, runtime multi-tenancy, is recommended against. Shape iii — one national deployment for the public surface, corpus and chatbot, with per-branch instances for members and visits — is flagged as attractive and genuinely undecided.

Refinement 2 · paused, not idle

The regenerative add-on

Target shape agreed: shape A, a group-defined add-on questionnaire and seal layered on the base certificate, assessed independently of the base outcome. Nothing can be built — the group has not written the criteria, so building the schema first repeats exactly the PGS_PASTURE_RAISED mistake D11 already recorded.

But three decisions are time-sensitive despite the pause, because another slice freezes each of them. They are the table below. Missing P1 costs a namespace retrofit over real evidence.

Part 7 — Recommendation

Six moves, in this order

The order is not the order of ambition. It is the order in which each move makes the next one cheaper, and it puts the only irreversible work (the template schema) after the only existential unknown (the spike).

  1. Commit and push what already exists

    Four reviewable commits, then set the remote. Nothing else on this list is safe until the corpus fix and the chatbot are in history rather than in a working tree.

    Anyone with the tree · half a session · unblocks everything

  2. Judge MVP 0 — the seven scenarios the suite cannot see

    Grounding (1), citation relevance (2), phone comfort (6), site voice (4), documents replacing Google Drive (7), no certification claim (8), and the column-corpus paper check (9). Book the three sign-off seats before the session, not after. Scenario 5 is not applicable.

    Product + PGS administrator + consumer member · one afternoon · closes MVP 0 or names its exceptions

  3. Fix the decision numbering, then renumber the two proposals

    D48 is free; D46 and D47 are taken. Record what the two refinement documents actually need as D48 and D49, apply the consequent spec edits in the same session, and regenerate the index once.

    Product · one hour · prevents a decision log that contradicts itself

  4. Ratify the embedding default, then force the re-ingest

    D20 ratification, then a re-ingest without --no-embed — the run must be forced, because ingestion is idempotent on sha256 and a plain re-run leaves zero vectors. ~340 chunks, four documents, one batch. Do it after D46 so vectors describe the corrected text.

    Product owner for the decision; a session for the run · turns lexical_only=True off

  5. Run the 0∥ spike — two days, throwaway code, a real phone in a real dead zone

    One question: does persisted storage survive a long visit on the assessor's actual device? The answer determines whether VisitSyncEnvelope v1 is enough or needs checkpointing. It is the only existential risk in the programme and the only thing standing between here and 1a sub-plan G.

    Whoever holds the assessor's device · two days · retires the largest unknown

  6. Then start MVP 1a at sub-plan B, with a second reader on the template

    Sub-plans A–F are ungated and ready; B is the irreversible one and the plan already flags the failure mode — 38 hand-transcribed questions where a partial template looks like a working one. Sequence B early and have the Crops template read by a second person before the first real visit freezes it as evidence.

    Build session + committee reader · the slice's main body of work

What would change this recommendation

If a UAT scenario fails on grounding or citation relevance, move 6 (start 1a) behind a prompt/instrumentation fix — a chatbot the group does not trust is worse than a later visit flow. If the spike comes back "storage does not survive", 1b is re-planned and only 1a ships; that is a scheduling change, not a design change, and it does not delay sub-plans A–F. If the group picks deployment shape iii (a national public surface), the deployment identity block becomes MVP 1a work rather than later — the one refinement that can pull forward.
