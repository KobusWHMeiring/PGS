C3, and the split

Settled 2026-10-31. C3 is the process shape: three horizontal cards, with Resources and Seals folded so the step body leads, the joining fee tied to Steps 1–2 and the certification fee to Step 3. The branch is two stacked cards in column three, one above the other, so the two paths are visible at once. Part 2b stays as the content-accurate alternative, recorded but not chosen.

Part 1 — C3 for the establishing table

The shape you picked. The one refinement kept from C3: the joining fee is a box under Steps 1–2 and the certification fee is a box under Step 3, so each amount stays with the step it qualifies.

Step 1 · Get together p. 4

Farmers who are already producing a surplus of organic produce and organized as an Organic farmer Association (OFO), and wanting to aggregate and sell their produce to a local market where there is consumer demand for organic produce, get together to form a PGS group.

Step 2 · Agree and establish p. 4

The PGS group agrees on operational procedures and their Basic Production Principles, based on an internationally accepted standard, against which farmers will be assessed. The members sign pledges and formally establish the PGS group at a general meeting.

The group conducts farm visits and on approval grants certificates to its farmer members in the relevant category. Farmers may label their products accordingly.

Fee — joining (Steps 1–2)

The local group determines the membership fee; annual membership R500 and once-off customisation R1 000 (paid to PGS SA).

Recommended annual sticker fee R350 (30% PGS SA / 70% group). Printing at own cost.

Note on the titles: Get together, Agree and establish, Visit and certify are reviewer-added — the paper prints only Step 1/2/3. The cards work without them; the titles are there to make the row scannable.

Part 2 — The split, in the third column

SAOSO is Step 1 · Step 2 · two paths: the PGS route ends with the “Organic PGS Certified” logo; the direct route goes to an Accredited Certifying Body. The third column holds both cards, stacked, with an or between them.

SAOSO — the two paths stacked in column three

Step 1 | Step 2 | [ Path A / or / Path B ], the two cards above one another.

Step 1 p. 7

For a minimum of 18 months, the PGS will be operating as per its agreed procedures and approach PGS SA for support. Farmers are assessed during farm visits and the PGS coordinator keeps a record of farm visits. Groups that have been compliant PGS SA members for this period can apply to SAOSO and PGS SA for approval to use the SAOSO “Organic PGS Certified” logo.

Resources
  • SAOSO application form
  • Appendix 9 of the SAOSO Standard
  • PGS SA Assessment of Group documents
Fee

Assessments by experienced PGS practitioners; fee determined case by case.

Step 2 p. 7

If approved by PGS SA the group is entitled to determine which of its farmers qualify to use the SAOSO “Organic PGS Certified” logo. Qualifying farmers sign a licensing agreement with SAOSO; the logo can be used for PGS-to-PGS trade.

Resources

Licensing with SAOSO: R500 farmer members / R2 500 retail members. Printing at the farmer’s cost.

What the split does: the two paths are visible at once, and the third column carries both without a fourth card. The dashed card borders and the or rule make it a fork, not two more steps. What it costs: Path A repeats the Step 2 outcome in summary (it is a pointer, not a second claim); the third column is taller than the first two; on a phone the split stacks after Step 2, so the two paths still read in order.

For

  • Both paths visible; no fourth card pretending to be a step.
  • The fork is drawn (dashed borders + or).
  • The same mechanism works for any future branch.

Against

  • Path A is a summary of Step 2; it can drift if Step 2 changes.
  • The third column is taller; the three columns are not equal.
  • Path A has no full text of its own — it points at Step 2.

What it gets right: the fork is at the point the paper makes it — whether you are in a PGS group. Path A visibly continues to Step 1; Path B ends in its own card. What it costs: Path A’s card is a short pointer, and the reader must look right to follow it; the first column is taller than the other two.

For

  • Content-accurate: the direct route is an alternative to joining, not a third step.
  • The two paths are the first thing a reader chooses between.
  • No repetition of Step 2’s outcome.

Against

  • The pointer card is thin; it spends a whole column on “continue right”.
  • The step numbers 1–2 now sit in columns two and three, which may read oddly.

Part 3 — The data the split needs

The split is a reviewed decision, not an inference. Two fields on the reviewed block are enough, and neither is a migration:

FieldWhereWhat it says
layout: processthe <!-- table --> headerThis table is a flow, not a grid (D77).
branch: 2 or branch: <row label>the same headerFrom this row on, the rows are an alternative path, not the next step. The renderer stacks them in the last column with an or.

Why not infer it? A row without a “Step” label is a hint, not a rule — and a future table may print a step without the word. The reviewer chooses the fork, exactly as they choose the legend. The production parser adds the two keys and the template branches on them; the corpus stays data.

The one claim-discipline risk in the split: Path A summarises Step 2’s outcome (the logo, the licence). Keep it a pointer — “see Step 2 for the licence” — rather than a second statement of the fees. D72.2 already refused a fee lifted out of its qualifying sentence; a summary card that repeats the amounts would be the same mistake in a new place.

Part 4 — The small decisions

  • Titles. Keep them as reviewed data (a title: list on the table) or drop them and print Step 1. The layout is identical; the titles buy scanning and cost a reviewed word each. My preference: keep them, reviewable, because a flow without names is harder to navigate.
  • Phone. The cards stack in order. In the third-column split, the two paths stack after Step 2 (correct). In the start split, the two paths come before Step 1 (also correct). No horizontal gesture either way.
  • Equal heights. The three cards are unequal by content; use align-items:start (as here) so a short card does not stretch, and let the split column be taller. The fee boxes sit under the row, not inside a card, so the columns do not have to match.
  • Page markers. Step 3 is on p5 (D72.5); the card shows it once, in its heading.

Part 5 — Your decision

The record of the decision: C3, Resources and Seals folded, the split in column three, the short titles kept. The controls below export that as a §18.3 row; change them only if the decision changes.
