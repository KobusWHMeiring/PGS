This is a decision document, not a grant application and not a status report. It exists to get one answer out of you in the next few days. Three rules apply to everything below:

Part 2 — The shape of the problem

The asymmetry: eligibility precedes merit

The review runs in a fixed order, and this is the single fact that drives the whole decision. Three of the four blockers in Part 1 are answered before a single point is scored. A project that fails them is marked ineligible and never read — the call says so plainly: "All projects that fail on any of these knock-out criteria, will not be further reviewed and will be marked ineligible."

Three consequences follow, and they are the whole argument:

  • The best engineering in this repo buys almost nothing. Technical excellence is 30% of the score. The repo's strength — 336 tests, an enforced import boundary, integer cents, explicit timeouts, a retrieval scope applied before ranking — is exactly the 30%, and a reviewer who cannot place the project on the stack never reaches it.
  • The call kills our headline. The grounded chatbot over the SAOSO standard is the most novel thing in the spec, and it is the one thing the call cannot fund. Whatever is proposed must be worth funding with the chatbot removed. The retrieval layer can stay — but as machinery, not as an "AI project".
  • The money pays for commons, not for finishing our product. Milestone payments arrive on delivery of work that is then published. "Fund the remaining MVP" is not a shape this fund accepts, however honest it is.

Part 3 — The options

Four shapes, each drawn as what a reviewer would actually see. The question is not "which is best for Outeniqua PGS" — it is "which of these is a building block someone else can pick up". Each option carries its own strongest case and its own weaknesses — they are the evidence section, deliberately side by side so no option gets a soft reading. In every diagram below, the outlined green box is the artifact the grant would pay for; every other box already exists or already happens. O1 has no outlined box, and that is the finding.

O1

Submit as-is: a digital platform for PGS organic certification

The whole monolith, framed as the project's own goal. No re-scoping; the proposal is the spec.

Strongest case for it
  • It is the truth. No re-framing to defend later, and nothing in the proposal that has to be delivered by someone else.
  • The call does invite it: "don't be afraid to send something completely different."
  • 336 tests and a documented architecture are more evidence of execution than most proposals carry — under-claiming with proof beats over-claiming without.
Its weaknesses
  • Fails "R&D as primary objective": the repo holds an implementation plan with numbered decisions, which reads as delivery.
  • Fails the 40% relevance axis. A certification system for one producer group is a vertical application; a reviewer placing it on a stack diagram puts it in "end user application for a niche", or nowhere.
  • Leaves the AI clause unaddressed while shipping gemini-embedding-001 in the retrieval path.
  • European dimension still fails. Re-scoping does not fix that one; only a partner does.
O2

The conformity engine: standards as data, not code

Fund the generalisation, not the group: an open engine where a certification scheme is a corpus plus a declarative schema, so a second standard, a second country or a second PGS group is data — never a migration.

Proven by a test, not a promise: a second standard is ingested with zero migrations, and the suite fails if a code change was needed.

Strongest case for it
  • The architecture already claims this. AGENTS.md makes "retarget without a migration" a rule, and spec D24 gave the corpus its own migration namespace on exactly this ground. We are not inventing a story; we are extracting the one we built.
  • It is a commons with a real proprietary counterweight: certification-management is sold as closed, per-seat SaaS. The alternative for a small producer group is paper.
  • Directly on two named axes: "creation and use of norms and standards" and "trust enhancing technologies".
  • Retargetability is demonstrable. A reviewer can run it. That is rare and it converts the 40% axis.
Its weaknesses
  • A reviewer may still file it as "an app for certifying farms", because the first instance is a farm-certification scheme and the pitch will keep saying so.
  • The R&D content is largely extraction and generalisation — which reads as refactoring unless the deliverables are new artifacts: a published schema, a format spec, and a second-standard proof.
  • No adopter yet. Stage 2 will ask who else uses it, and "the group we built it for" is the same answer as O1.
  • Redistribution rights on the corpus are unverified (Part 1).
O3

Fund the part that does not exist yet: offline capture, merge on reconnect, evidence reconciliation — as a reusable client-side library and a documented sync protocol, not as one PWA.

Strongest case for it
  • The call names it: "'local-first' infrastructure". No interpretation needed, which is worth a lot when 40% is relevance.
  • Genuinely hard, genuinely reusable: conflict resolution over evidentiary records, partial sync, media on a flaky link. Every field-inspection regime has this problem and none of them solved it well.
  • Naturally FOSS-complete — a JS library plus a protocol spec has no proprietary dependency to explain away.
  • It has a concrete adversary: the alternative in the field is paper, and paper does not lose data.

  • You would be entering a field with incumbents (Automerge/Yjs/CRDT toolkits, existing offline-first sync stacks). Stage 2 always asks "what is the difference in approach to existing projects U, V and W" — and the answer would have to be written from scratch, by 3 November.
  • Loses the one thing this repo has proven, which is governance-grade domain modelling.
O4

Verifiable attestations for certification claims

Fund the trust layer: a signed, scannable attestation of a certification outcome, plus the publication rule that only currently-true claims can resolve — as an open profile others can implement.

Strongest case for it
  • The best story in the repo: the group's own rules already forbid over-claiming, and test_claim_discipline.py enforces it in code. "Only currently-true claims can resolve" is a verifiable-claims problem with a working precedent behind it.
  • Cross-domain by construction. Any inspection regime, any certification body, any attestation has the same shape.
  • Squarely inside "trust enhancing technologies" + "norms and standards".
Its weaknesses
  • Nothing to attest yet. No FarmVisit, no Assessment, no Certificate model exists. The layer would be specified against an object that has not been built.
  • Crowded: W3C Verifiable Credentials, SD-JWT VC, Open Badges 3.0 and the EU Digital Identity Wallet all occupy this ground. A new format is not fundable; an implementation proving the format works for a sector nobody serves is.
  • Best used as a milestone inside O2, where the outcome model is being built anyway. Alone, it is a smaller, weaker O2.

Part 4 — What the call says, and where it disagrees with itself

Read it as an argument, not a rulebook

The call page contains real tensions. Naming them is the difference between a proposal that anticipates the objection and one that walks into it.

What it saysWhat it pulls towardWhere it rubs
"We are open to new ideas… don't be afraid to send something completely different." Novel, unexpected contributions "The focus of Restack is on securing the core infrastructure and foundational technologies on which global society critically depends." Novelty is welcome; category is not negotiable. This is the tension O1 walks into.
"There are no categorical exclusions of persons who may not receive support." Open to anyone, anywhere "It is a knock-out criterion… 'European dimension'… at least 60% of the funds." Who may apply is open; where the money lands is not. Solve it with a partner, not with an argument.
"This is really an open call." Anything that helps the stack "AI-related projects are not within scope — unless… >1 million active human users." Our retrieval and chatbot sit inside the excluded category, and we cannot claim a million users. Remove it from the headline.
"between 5.000 and 50.000 EUR" Small, self-contained first grant "with the possibility to scale them up significantly if there is proven potential" — 150k per proposal, 500k lifetime, but scale-up needs a successfully concluded smaller grant first. This supports a two-grant strategy rather than one ambitious ask.
"research and development as their primary objective" R&D, not maintenance The eligible-activities list includes packaging, documentation, WCAG compliance and security audits. Maintenance work is fundable inside an R&D project — which is where our accessibility and packaging gaps belong.
"The Open Internet Stack needs to be inclusive… taking care of accessibility is, as far as we are concerned, the 'new normal'." WCAG as a deliverable They fund a HAN accessibility audit and will condition payment on audit outcomes above 50k. DESIGN.md already mandates ≥44 px targets — but no audit exists, so this is a genuine, low-cost deliverable we can promise with a straight face.

How the tie breaks. This is a fund for infrastructure, assessed by infrastructure people, with knock-outs they cannot waive. So: eligibility beats relevance beats quality. Any option that needs the reviewer to accept a redefinition of "infrastructure" is starting from behind, however good the code is.

Part 6 — Recommendation

The recommendation

Submit O2 — the standard-agnostic conformity engine — for 5–50k EUR, with verifiable attestations as one of its milestones and the offline layer explicitly deferred to a second, later proposal.

Do not submit O1. It is honest and it fails three knock-outs before anyone reads it.

Why O2 and not O3

O3 is the more elegant project and, if it already existed, the better one. It does not exist — and a proposal written in the next fortnight would ask a reviewer to fund 100% of an unproven codebase, in a language this repo contains none of, against incumbents. O2 asks them to fund the part where we can already show our work: 336 tests, a corpus with its own migration namespace, a scope applied before ranking, and a written rule that retargeting must not require a migration.

The one thing that makes O2 fundable rather than refactoring is a second standard and a second adopter. Everything else is secondary.

Preconditions — in order, before any writing

  • Add a LICENSE. One hour. The call requires FOSS in its entirety; today ls LICENSE* fails. AGPL-3.0 or Apache-2.0 depending on whether you want hosted-service reciprocity.
  • Push the repo public. The remote has never been pushed. Reviewers verify claims; a private repo makes every claim in the proposal unfalsifiable, which reads as unverifiable.
  • Add CI. A workflow running the suite is the cheapest possible proof of the "336 tests" sentence — and their FAQ sells "setup and design of software testing and continuous integration" as fundable activity, so the gap is also a legitimate line item.
  • Get an EU co-applicant taking ≥60% of the funds. This is the schedule-risk item and it is not technical. Without it, nothing else matters. Start with it today.
  • Decide the embedding story. Not "remove Gemini" — the lexical floor already stands without a key, so the honest line is "the engine degrades to FTS and says so; a self-hosted embedding provider is a stretch goal." Rehearse that sentence.

Also worth doing this week, whatever you decide

  • Attend the NLnet office hour on Wednesday 28 October — six days before the deadline, in their Matrix room. They explicitly offer it, they will not pre-review a proposal, but "is a sectoral conformity engine in scope" is exactly the question the hour exists for. It is the cheapest way to de-risk the 40% axis.
  • Ask a second PGS group or PGS-SA whether they would adopt a shared engine. One sentence of interest from a real adopter is worth more than any paragraph of architectural justification. It is also the honest test of whether O2 is a commons or a rationalisation.
  • Note the cadence. Missing 3 November costs two months, not a year — the next call is early January. A weak submission is worse than waiting: the FAQ is clear that a rejection is not a black mark, but the time spent is.

What would change my mind

  • No EU co-applicant by ~28 October — then do not submit this cycle. O2 without eligibility is a wasted fortnight; spend it getting the partner instead, or on the second-standard proof, so the January call is strong.
  • NLnet says at the office hour that sectoral applications are out of scope — then O3 becomes the recommendation, because "local-first infrastructure" is the call's own vocabulary and needs no advocacy.
  • The SAOSO/OPP texts may not be redistributed — O2 survives, but M2's proof changes shape: the second standard must be one we may ship. Verify before writing the milestone, not after.
  • A second adopter commits — O2's relevance row strengthens enough that I would raise the ask toward 50k and consider pulling the attestation milestone forward.
  • If you would rather build than write — that is a legitimate answer, and it points at the January call with M1 and M2 already done. It is not a wasted two months; it is the same work, de-risked.
