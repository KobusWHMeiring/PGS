G11 · Option E, explained · an explainer, not a proposal

E keeps the words where the paper put them, and moves the meaning to where the reader needs it.

Today the assistant cannot say “R1 500” for the lowest turnover band, because the row it retrieves is three numbers with no labels, and the audit refuses a quote the row does not contain. Option E fixes that without adding a single character to the corpus: the model is shown the table as a table, quotes cells only, and the column heading is attached when the citation is drawn.

Companion to g11_row_labels_exploration.html (the decision) and g12_ingestion_generalisation.html Measured on the dev corpus, 401 chunks, 2026-11-01/02

0

How to read this

This page teaches one mechanism. It does not re-argue the five options — that is the decision page, and its recommendation stands. It exists because E is the only option whose idea is structural (“a citation can display context it does not quote”), and structural ideas are hard to picture from a one-line description.

Nine short steps, each one answering the question the previous step raises. Every claim that could be checked against the repo was, and is quoted with a file:line. Where something is a judgement rather than a measurement, it says so.

The one-sentence version. A printed table has three layers — what the text says, what it means, and what the reader is shown — and today the assistant forces all three through one channel (the chunk text). E gives each layer its own channel: the corpus keeps the characters, the prompt carries the structure, the citation carries the context.
1

The paper prints a grid. The corpus stores a row.

OPP §2.3 is the assessment fee structure: three columns, six bands. Here is what the paper prints, from the reviewed artefact knowledge/corpus/opp.md:

What the paper prints (and the reader draws)

Annual farm turnover (gross sales) Initial certification fee (R) Renewal fee (R)
Below R 250,0001,500900
R 250,001 – R 500,0002,5002,000
R 500,001 – R 1,000,0003,0002,500
… three more bands……

Legend, in the artefact: “Amounts are in rand, printed without a thousands separator.” That legend exists because 1,500 is ambiguous — and it is the same problem the assistant has, in a different place.

What the corpus stores as one chunk

DocumentChunk.text Below R 250,000
1,500
900

One chunk per reviewed row, cells joined by a newline (knowledge/sections/__init__.py, ParsedTableRow.text). The heading is not in it. The paper prints the heading once, above the grid; the corpus prints it zero times.

That is the whole defect, and it is smaller than it sounds: 6 of 44 rows are affected. Appendix 2 and Appendix 6a's 30 rows are prose (“a cell is a sentence”), and the landscape's 8 rows carry their own printed label inside each cell. Only the OPP's six fee bands are bare numbers.

2

So the model receives three numbers and has to guess

The prompt renders each retrieved chunk as a block. From core/services/_chatbot.py:1433: 

LOCATOR: §2.3
Document: Outeniqua PGS Principles, Policies and Procedures, page 12
Numbered sub-clauses available: none - omit `sub_ref` for this passage.
Below R 250,000
1,500
900

Nothing here says which number is the initial fee and which is the renewal. The model is then asked for an answer plus citations, and every citation carries two things the model supplies: 

// from the schema in the prompt, and `audit_citations`' input
{
  "answer": "...",
  "citations": [ { "section_ref": "§2.3", "sub_ref": null, "quote": "..." } ]
}

The quote is the load-bearing field. Everything the reader sees as evidence comes from it, and it is checked by a substring test.

3

Why the obvious sentence is refused: the audit is not being pedantic

Suppose the model works out that 900 is the renewal fee and writes the sentence the reader wants. Here is the check that decides its fate (core/services/_chatbot.py:1055-1061): 

quote_key = _normalise_text(quote)
# a quote is accepted only if it is a substring of the cited chunk's own text
candidates = [chunk for chunk in candidates if quote_key in _normalise_text(chunk.text)]
problems.append(f"{ref}: the quote is not in the cited chunk")

_normalise_text normalises whitespace only — “punctuation and case stay exact” (:816). So the failure is not fuzzy matching; the sentence is simply absent from the chunk.

model writes “Renewal fee R900” the sentence the reader needs
→check
audit not in chunk text the chunk says 1,500 and 900, never “renewal”
→consequence
whole answer refused “one invented citation makes the rest untrustworthy” — it is not trimmed

And this is not hypothetical. It was measured with the best possible input. The team added D73's rendition — a labelled line stored alongside the chunk — and put it in the prompt. The model read the labels perfectly and quoted them: 

what the model was told:
Annual farm turnover (gross sales): Below R 250,000 · Initial certification fee (R): 1,500 · ...

what the model did:
ANSWER: ... a farmer with turnover between R 500,001 and R 1,000,000 pays a renewal fee of R 2,500
AUDIT:  REFUSED   ! §2.3: the quote is not in the cited chunk
This is the crux of E. The model was not wrong about the world — it was right, and the contract refused it. So the fix cannot be “help the model understand”. The fix has to be where the label lives: out of the quote, in the citation.

One more wall, from the same room. D69 says the corpus is the document's characters — nothing substituted or added. That is why the team could not simply have the ingest write “Renewal fee:” in front of 900. Any option that puts meaning into the chunk text is spending something.

4

Option A — the obvious fix, and what it costs

A says: paste the printed heading line into every row's chunk text. It works — the sentence becomes quotable and the row becomes findable — and it is small.

What the paper prints

one heading, once
Annual farm turnover … | Initial … | Renewal …
R 250,001 – R 500,000  |  2,500  |  2,000

What A's corpus prints

six chunks, each carrying it
Annual … | Initial … | Renewal …
R 250,001 – R 500,000 / 2,500 / 2,000

and again, above the next band,
and again, above the next band …

Three things follow, and all three are real:

  • A citation may now quote a line that is not contiguous on the page — the audit passes it because it compares against the chunk, but D69's “the corpus is the characters” has loosened.
  • A reader viewing one row's passage sees a heading above it that the paper does not print there.
  • Chunk text changes, so two documents must be re-ingested and two tests that pin the current text must change.

That is acceptable as a stopgap. It is a bad target, because the corpus now carries a rule built for the smallest case in it.

5

E's idea: three layers, three channels

Here is the part that is hard to see from a one-line description. A table row, in this system, is three different things at once — and every previous option tried to satisfy all three with the same bytes.

Finding what retrieval ranks
The embedding never has to be the paper's characters — D73 settled that: quote from text, embed a rendition. Structure can help here without any risk, because nothing here is ever shown as evidence. 
# free to be anything:
table 2.3 PGS assessment fee structure
columns: turnover | initial fee | renewal fee
Quoting what the audit checks
The quote must be the paper's own characters, contiguous, inside the cited chunk. This is the only layer D69 actually governs. 
Below R 250,000
1,500
900  <- this is what may be quoted
Showing what the reader sees
Context is attached when the citation is drawn, from the artefact — exactly the way a citation already renders a document title and a page number, neither of which the model supplied either. 
§2.3 — Below R 250,000 · initial R1 500 · renewal R900

E is the only option that gives each layer its own channel. A moves the heading into the quoting layer (it becomes quotable, and then must be repeated). B collapses quoting and showing into one passage (losing row granularity). D leaves the corpus entirely and grounds the answer in code. E is the one that mirrors the artefact the paper already is: the heading is context, the cell is the quotation.

The conceptual unlock, stated once. A citation today is a quotation. E makes a citation a quotation plus a frame — and the two must look different on screen, or a reader could believe the paper prints the label beside the row. That UI distinction is the work, not an afterthought.

Cells only. The model is free to explain in the answer; only the quote is checked.

3 The audit — unchanged

PASS  "Below R 250,000\n1,500\n900"
       is a substring of the row chunk

No new rule. Still quote in chunk.text. E does not weaken the audit; it stops asking the model to quote something the paper never printed on that line.

Why E is cheaper than “a new citation category” sounds

The decision page calls E the biggest change of the five, and that is true of the prompt contract. It is not true of the infrastructure, and this is the finding that changes the estimate: the reader already does exactly this lookup, in production, today.

The three pieces already exist

  • knowledge/tables.py:716 — ArtefactGrid already holds columns, labels (the printed label of each row) and rows (cells in printed column order). That is precisely frame 5's data.
  • knowledge/tables.py:790 — align_artefact_grid(grid, texts) maps each artefact row to the passage that holds it, whitespace-normalised, and returns None rather than a mislabelled grid when they disagree.
  • core/services/_documents.py:640 — _artefact_grid() resolves an open branch to its reviewed section and draws the grid. Its comment records that §2.3 already works through this path: “The OPP's §2.3 is the first table in a numbered document”, matched by section_ref.

Citation already carries section_ref, sub_ref, document, document_code, page and reading_anchor — every field the renderer needs to find the grid. The artefact's review metadata already names the row (<!-- row: Below R 250,000 -->), which ParsedTableRow.label_text exposes.

So E's genuinely new work is three things, and only one of them is hard:

  1. The prompt shape — the model must be shown a table it may not quote from. Medium: it is a contract change with its own prompt version, and the “cells only” rule needs a test that a model which ignores it is caught.
  2. Row identity on the citation — small: one field, resolved through align_artefact_grid, which already fails closed.
  3. “Context, not quotation” in the UI — the hard one, and it is a design question, not a plumbing one. If it cannot be made legible, E's case collapses (see step 9).

What E does not fix: the row has to be retrieved first

Quotability is one of two failures. The other is that the band row never reaches the top eight passages. Measured, from the decision page:

+fuse

So E is half the answer, and the other half is the same under every option. The selection fix — reserving the semantic seat properly, and not letting the alias arm truncate the tail (knowledge/services.py:660) — has to land whichever option you pick. “No similarity ranking chooses a band.” Plan E as selection fix → E's prompt and row identity → the UI distinction, in that order.
9

The honest risks, and what would make me withdraw E

Three costs, none of them hidden

  • A citation now displays text the passage does not contain. That is a new category, and it is a real risk to the product's central promise (“every sentence traceable to the corpus”). The mitigation is the UI distinction — and if reviewers find it confusing, the mitigation has failed.
  • The citation is coupled to a reviewed file at read time. Fine here (the reader already does it), but a citation can no longer be rendered from the chunk alone, so a missing artefact degrades the citation to a plain quote — which is exactly today's behaviour, so the failure is safe.
  • Rows need a stable identity. A chunk id will not survive a re-ingest. The reviewed row label will, if align_artefact_grid's whitespace-normalised join holds — and where it does not, it already falls back rather than mislabelling.
What would change my mind. If E's “context, not quotation” distinction cannot be made legible in the answer UI — if a reader cannot tell which words the paper printed and which were drawn from structure — then E trades one dishonesty for another, and A is the right answer, accepting the repetition and the loosened D69.

What would add to it. If you want the R1 500 floor guaranteed regardless of retrieval, add D as well — accepting that one sentence of the answer is grounded in a reviewed constant rather than a passage. That is a contract change, and it should be a decision of its own, not a side effect.

The separate measurement neither option escapes

The prose rendition (Appendix 2's rows) was reverted after three suite questions refused, and the revert is not yet justified. Re-read carefully, the evidence says: absolute similarity drops slightly every time, but the order changes — and for the manure question the top row moves from the wrong row (0.665) to the right one (0.617), with second place also 0.617. A tie decides it. And one of the three failures was noise (ducks refuses about one run in two with the revert in place, the pre-existing G1 class).

“The rendition changes the order; whether it helps is not established.” Settling it costs about 40 model calls (4 questions × 5 runs × 2 configurations, recording failure class rather than verdict). Run it before the next corpus slice. It gates A directly; it informs E through the finding layer only, which is why E is the safer bet while it is open.

Your decision

Answered 2026-11-02: E (spec §18.3 D79). The product owner chose the target alone — A is not to be built. The three build slices are the reserved semantic seat, then the prompt shape and row identity, then the “context, not quotation” UI distinction.
