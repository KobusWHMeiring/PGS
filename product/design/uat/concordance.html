How to use this page

This page does not check anything. It records your judgement - that is the whole point of a UAT item, and why the question comes before the steps.

  1. Run the preflight below. If a blocking row fails, stop and fix the instance.
  2. Fill in your name and role once, then use Use these details on every scenario.
  3. Run each scenario in the app, then choose PASS, FAIL or N/A. FAIL and N/A require a reason.
  4. Press Export sign-off and send the file to whoever writes it back.

Everything autosaves in this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere: no network request is made by this page.

1

Arriving at the line

The judgement — the question you are answering

Does following the link feel like arriving at the clause itself — can you put your finger on the sentence you just read in the answer — or does it feel like being dropped near it? And, with the tab open: is it obvious the answer is still there, or did opening a tab lose the reader's place? A yes to the first and a no to the second is a pass.

Setup — do this first

an approved answer page whose citation you have not yet followed. Action: follow View this line.

Expected

the reader opens in a new tab, on the branch containing the quoted clause, with the passage visibly marked, the tree showing that branch as the one you are in, and the answer page still open behind it.

8

A clause reads as the document prints it

The judgement — the question you are answering

Does this read as a reproduction of the paper, or as a machine's attempt at one? The markers are the browser's list markers, not characters added to the text — the corpus prints a bare a — and whether that reads as honest is a human call. A reader who has the PDF open should be able to follow the same clause in both without losing their place.

Setup — do this first

open /documents/saoso-standard-v2.1/read/?section=s5-1-3 (Animal Husbandry, the conditions in 5.1.3). Then open ?section=s8-1 (Mushroom Production), which has a list inside a list.

Expected

5.1.3's conditions read as a lettered list — a. to f., each condition a row, a wrapped condition continuing under its own marker — and the sentences before and after read as paragraphs rather than as a ladder of lines. In 8.1, the roman i./ii. sits inside item a. The page's note at the foot says the words and punctuation are the document's and that line breaks are reflowed.

9

The appendix reads as the paper's table

The judgement — the question you are answering

Does a substance read as an entry with a condition attached to it — the thing a farmer looks up — or as a list of standalone inputs? And specifically: does a row with no condition read as the paper declining to permit something, or does it read as a blank the site forgot to fill? If the second, the label or the legend is wrong, not the data.

Setup — do this first

open /documents/saoso-standard-v2.1/read/?section=appendix-2. Have the PDF open at printed p. 76 beside it.

Expected

a caption naming the paper's two columns, one sentence saying what the appendix is, then 30 entries — each a substance on its own line with its condition below it. Four part headings (I. Plant and animal origin … IV. Others) and three South African specific qualifiers break the list where the paper breaks it, so the Farmyard manure row that appears twice does so under different conditions rather than as a duplicate. The 19 rows whose condition cell the paper leaves empty carry a muted no condition printed rather than silence, and no row reads as permission.

Action

read the branch top to bottom, as a reader looking for whether a substance may be used.

A reference goes somewhere

The judgement — the question you are answering

Does each link land on what the sentence meant — and where it cannot, does it say so rather than guessing? The Part A case is the one to judge hardest: a reader who lands on Organic Ecosystems (Section 2) instead of an explanation has been told something false by a link. A definition is a click away

The judgement — the question you are answering

Does following a term answer the question the reader had — "what does this word mean here?" — without losing the clause they were reading? And: does the clause still read as a clause with the links in it, or is it now link-studded? This is the judgement that decides whether the feature stays.

Setup — do this first

?section=s22 at 22.1.7, which uses ACB; and ?section=s4 at 4.2, which uses Conversion Period. The glossary (?section=s1) open in another tab.

Expected

each term opens the glossary in a new tab, at the definition itself — not at the top of the branch — with the definition visible without scrolling. Within the glossary, terms are not linked to themselves. Elsewhere, each term is linked once per passage at most, not on every occurrence.

Action

follow ACB from 22.1.7, then Conversion Period from 4.2; then scroll the glossary and read the definitions of ACB, Habitat and Extensive Farming in place.
