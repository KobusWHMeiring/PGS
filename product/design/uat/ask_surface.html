How to use this page

This page does not check anything. It records your judgement - that is the whole point of a UAT item, and why the question comes before the steps.

  1. Run the preflight below. If a blocking row fails, stop and fix the instance.
  2. Fill in your name and role once, then use Use these details on every scenario.
  3. Run each scenario in the app, then choose PASS, FAIL or N/A. FAIL and N/A require a reason.
  4. Press Export sign-off and send the file to whoever writes it back.

Everything autosaves in this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere: no network request is made by this page.

Pre-test setup

  1. Confirm the instance can be judged at all: open /uat/ (staff login) and press the buttons, or run python manage.py uat_seed --check. It reports the corpus (chunks and vectors), the published documents and the extraction tool. If it blocks, stop — a judgement made against a half-seeded instance is not evidence. (An empty corpus is rebuilt per document, e.g. python manage.py ingest_standard --doc saoso --file "pgs_docs/saoso-version-2.1-2026.pdf"; there is no --all flag. GEMINI_API_KEY must be set, because a grounded answer is what produces a real card.) 2. Start the instance: python manage.py runserver, or use the deployed https://organicguarantee.africa after it has this commit. 3. Log in to /admin/ as a user holding PGS administrator — the approval step is the point of the board and must be judged by the person who does it. 4. Have one other person available for Scenario 4 (a second reader, not a developer).

1

A card a stranger can read

The judgement — the question you are answering

Is the question safe to publish as it stands — no third party named, no farm identified, no personal detail — and is the answer text something this group is willing to stand behind at a permanent URL? A concise "yes, publish" is the call; per-farm detail gets edited out before approval, not explained afterwards.

Setup — do this first

ask "Can I use cardboard from my neighbour?" on /ask/ with the consent box ticked. In /admin/, open the card. Action: read the question and the answer as they would appear, then approve. Open /ask/ and the card's page.

Expected

the card's question reads as a question a person typed, and its page answers it in sentences you would repeat to a farmer.

2

The refusal stays a boundary

The judgement — the question you are answering

Does the Waiting card read as the group drawing a boundary confidently, or as something having gone wrong? This is the product's central claim (SPEC §13); a page that reads as a malfunction fails even though every test passes.

Setup — do this first

from the same instance, ask a question the corpus cannot answer (e.g. "Do I need EU organic certification to export to Germany?"), consent box ticked.

Expected

the refusal is styled warm, not red, and it appears on the board as a Waiting card that names the administrator as the next step. Its page explains that the document does not answer it.

3

Landing on the clause

The judgement — the question you are answering

Does the landing feel like arriving at the clause in its context, or like being dropped into the middle of a wall of text? The anchor can be asserted; whether a reader can tell where they are and what surrounds the rule cannot.

Setup — do this first

open an approved card's page. Action: follow one citation.

Expected

the document reader opens at the clause, the passage is visually marked, and its printed page and section reference are visible without scrolling far.

4

The citation survives leaving the site

The judgement — the question you are answering

Judged by the second person alone: could they file this in a FORM, or repeat it to a neighbour, without the page being open? If they have to add a caveat or ask what a section number means, the citation is not finished.

Setup — do this first

hand the screen to the second person (not a developer). Ask them to copy the citation block from an answer page and paste it into a message to a colleague.

Expected

the pasted text names the document, the section and the page, and quotes the clause verbatim.

5

390 px, and a slow connection

The judgement — the question you are answering

On a phone in a farm office, is the composer the obvious next action, and is any text clipped, cramped or pushed below the fold? Layout at width is a judgement; overflow can be measured, legibility cannot.

Setup — do this first

browser at 390 px wide, throttled to slow 3G. Open /ask/, an answer page, and the SAOSO reader.

Expected

the composer is the first thing on /ask/; cards stack one per row; the reader's contents list is reachable without a horizontal scroll; the clause text is legible at that width.
