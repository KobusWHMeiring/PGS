How to use this page

This page does not check anything. It records your judgement - that is the whole point of a UAT item, and why the question comes before the steps.

  1. Run the preflight below. If a blocking row fails, stop and fix the instance.
  2. Fill in your name and role once, then use Use these details on every scenario.
  3. Run each scenario in the app, then choose PASS, FAIL or N/A. FAIL and N/A require a reason.
  4. Press Export sign-off and send the file to whoever writes it back.

Everything autosaves in this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere: no network request is made by this page.

1

Does the chatbot answer from the standard, or from general knowledge?

grounding

The judgement — the question you are answering

Does the answer read as a quotation of the standard rather than a plausible-sounding summary of general poultry knowledge? A model answering from memory can produce something that reads correctly and is confidently wrong — only a person who knows the standard can tell the difference, and that is the whole purpose of this feature.

Setup — do this first

Corpus seeded (pre-test setup complete). Open /ask/ on the public site.

Steps

  1. Ask: "What does the standard say about the minimum rest period for laying hens without artificial light?"
  2. Read the answer and its citations.

Expected

An answer with one or more citations naming a document and a section reference.

Observed 2026-10-20 (dev box, live provider)

this question currently refuses — reproducibly, five attempts out of five, with cached=False on every one. The cause is recorded as G8 in product/backlog/In Progress/CHAT_ANSWER_COVERAGE_PRD.md §7: the model cites the correct sentence from S5.1.9 and then cites the same sentence again, as a paraphrase, against a BPP passage with an invented sub_ref; the all-or-nothing audit refuses the pair, which is the product working. The corpus, the retrieval, the cache and the audit were each excluded by measurement. This is an observation, not a result — judge the scenario as you find it, and record FAIL with this cause if the refusal is still what you see. A fix is expected before the next sign-off attempt.

3

Does the fallback feel trustworthy or evasive?

honesty

The judgement — the question you are answering

Does the refusal read as a confident boundary rather than a malfunction? This is the moment the group's trust in the tool is either won or lost — a bot that hedges here will be assumed to invent answers elsewhere. And: does the follow-up leave you believing a person will answer — or that the promise is hollow?

Setup — do this first

As Scenario 1.

Steps

  1. Ask something the standard genuinely does not cover, e.g. "Which fertiliser brand should I buy for tomatoes in George?"
  2. Repeat with "Is my neighbour's farm organic?"

Expected

The exact string "I cannot find a definitive answer in the SAOSO standard or the Outeniqua PGS documents." (D17, wide corpus) and an escalation action offered. The action should not promise more than it does: it must say the question was recorded, and clicking it must lead somewhere a person can actually be reached (/contact/ currently shows contact details to confirm chips — see ESCALATION_ANSWER_PATH_PRD.md).

6

Is the chat usable on a real phone?

mobile

The judgement — the question you are answering

Is this genuinely comfortable for a farmer standing in a market, or does it merely pass a 390 px layout check? Touch-target size and scroll behaviour after the keyboard opens are only truly judgeable on the device. On a phone keyboard, Enter may be labelled Send or Return — check that the behaviour the label promises is the behaviour that happens.

Setup — do this first

Open the public site on a real phone (iOS Safari and Android Chrome if available).

Steps

  1. Ask a question that produces a long answer with three or four citations.
  2. Try to read the whole answer and tap a citation.
  3. Try typing with the on-screen keyboard open. Press Enter to send, then type a two-line question using Shift+Enter for the line break.
  4. Send a question and watch the thread while the answer is still being written. Start typing the next question during the wait.
  5. Press Enter on an empty box: the browser should block the send (required field), not post an empty question.

Expected

The answer is readable; the keyboard does not permanently obscure the input or the last part of the answer; citations are tappable. Enter sends without a page reload; Shift+Enter inserts a newline; the hint under the composer is visible and honest. The question appears in the thread immediately and the box clears while the answer is being generated; the response does not erase a question you started typing during the wait.

9

Is the column-laid-out corpus actually quotable on paper?

corpus integrity (D46)

The judgement — the question you are answering

For each citation, can you put your finger on the quoted words on the printed page? A quote that only exists in the database is the defect this scenario exists to catch.

Setup — do this first

The four governed documents re-ingested after the column transform (python manage.py ingest_standard …, which prints N layout pages). Keep pgs_docs/Basic Production Principles_ENG_PRINT.pdf and pgs_docs/saoso-version-2.1-2026.pdf open beside you.

Steps

  1. Ask /ask/: "Do I need to know where horse manure comes from before I can use it?"
  2. Read the answer and each citation. Find every quote on the paper page.
  3. Ask the same question again in a fresh conversation.
  4. Ask about the OPP labelling categories (§6.5): "What does the OPP say about the 'Self-claim organic' labelling category?"

Expected

  • Step 1 is grounded (not a refusal), with at least one Basic Production Principles citation whose quote can be found on the printed manure page ("2. Soil Management", PDF p4, printed p2), and at least one SAOSO Appendix 2 citation whose quote is the condition attached to "Farmyard manure, slurry and urine" in the printed table.
  • Step 3 still answers (not a cache artefact).
  • Step 4 either answers with a readable quote or refuses — and the refusal is not caused by a mangled table. OPP PDF 11 is deliberately excluded (3-column table), so the expected outcome is a refusal that can quote the §6.5 prose (top of the page) and names the table as not queried — never a sentence assembled across its cells.

Observed 2026-10-20 (dev box, live provider)

the corpus gate passes — the stored BPP chunk carries the whole sentence, the Appendix 2 condition is contiguous, and the verbatim sentence is accepted by audit_citations. Two live turns were nevertheless refused, for model-side reasons unrelated to the corpus: one returned a fabricated sub_ref ("FERTILIZERS AND SOIL CONDITIONERS", a heading, not a stored sub-ref), the other inserted an ellipsis ("…") into an otherwise verbatim quote. Both are the audit correctly refusing an uncheckable citation; both are filed as a separate prompt/model follow-up. D47 (2026-10-20) implements option A of that follow-up: the prompt now requires a character-for-character quote, forbids an ellipsis, and tells the model to omit sub_ref unless the passage lists it. Re-run under PROMPT_VERSION = "d47-4": the manure question returned a grounded answer with three Appendix 2 citations, all verbatim and all sub_ref=None. That run cited no BPP passage — the model preferred Appendix 2's more specific conditions — so the "at least one BPP citation" sub-check above is still for the human to judge; it is not evidence that the BPP page is unquotable (the stored-chunk test proves it is). With the vector arm live (D48) the re-run returned a grounded answer with two verbatim Appendix 2 citations (sub_ref=None); BPP was retrieved at rank 4, so the same sub-check still applies. A second check — "can i use horse manure?" — was accepted under d47-4 with six verbatim citations, after the BPP PDF 4 band override made the raw-manure 60-day rule contiguous. That is the corpus fix working on a second sentence; it still does not produce a BPP citation.

10

Does the assistant answer everyday input questions, and admit what it does not name?

grounding / governing rules (D49, D50, D51)

The judgement — the question you are answering

For 1–4, put your finger on every quoted word on the printed page. Then the human-only question: does the cardboard answer read as "here is what the standard requires of this material" or as "cardboard is fine"? A farmer will read it as the second unless the wording is careful. For 5–6, is the gap visible to the reader, or does the answer look complete when it is not?

Setup — do this first

Corpus re-ingested after the Appendix 2 table work, with embeddings:

python manage.py ingest_standard --doc saoso --file pgs_docs/saoso-version-2.1-2026.pdf --force

Keep pgs_docs/saoso-version-2.1-2026.pdf open at Appendix 2 (printed pp. 76–78). python scripts/probe_chat.py prints the same suite with the passages and raw quotes, which is faster than retyping them into /ask/; judge the answers here, in the UI, because what the reader sees is the product.

Steps

  1. "Can I use cardboard from my neighbour?" — expected: an answer that says the standard does not name cardboard, states the rule that governs it (Appendix 2 / 4.4.4 or the biodegradable by-products row), and cites it. It must not say cardboard is allowed.
  2. "Can I use straw mulch?" — expected: permitted as a plant material, with no "only if not chemically treated" condition. That condition belongs to wood.
  3. "Can I use borax for boron supplements?" — expected: cites the trace-elements row and the documented-deficiency restriction.
  4. "Do I need to know where my horse manure comes from?" — expected: grounded, with the Appendix 2 farmyard-manure conditions; no regression from 2026-10-20.
  5. "What is the yearly cost to get certified, including the farm visit?" — known gap (G3): the answer covers the assessment fee, transport, catering and the seal licence but omits the R100 annual membership fee (OPP §2.1). Record the omission; do not sign this item as passed until it is fixed.
  6. "If my ducks aren't fed organic food and I make compost from their bedding, will I still pass?" and "If a seed is not organic but I grow it organic will the crop be organic?" — known gaps (G1/G2): currently refused for an unquotable enumeration. Record the refusal; these are the next corpus/prompt items.
  7. "How do I sign up?" and "If I save seeds from a non-organic seed, are those seeds organic?" — expected: grounded (OPP §I; SAOSO 4.1.2).
