The four principles of organic agriculture

SAOSO's standard opens with the IFOAM–Organics International principles, and says they "are to be used as a whole". Each is quoted here from the standard itself.

Health

Organic agriculture should sustain and enhance the health of soil, plant, animal, human and planet as one and indivisible.

SAOSO S2.1

Ecology

Organic agriculture should be based on living ecological systems and cycles, work with them, emulate them and help sustain them.

SAOSO S2.2

Fairness

Organic agriculture should build on relationships that ensure fairness with regard to the common environment and life opportunities.

SAOSO S2.3

Care

Organic agriculture should be managed in a precautionary and responsible manner to protect the health and well-being of current and future generations and the environment.

SAOSO S2.4

The six basic elements of a Participatory Guarantee System

What makes a group a PGS rather than a certificate bought from an agency.

{% include "public/sections/_element_cards.html" %}

“Participatory Guarantee Systems are locally focused quality assurance systems. They certify producers based on active participation of stakeholders and are built on a foundation of trust, social networks and knowledge exchange.”

— IFOAM–Organics International, quoted in the SA Organic Certification Landscape

Three ways trust is established

A PGS is a second-party system. It exists because a first-party claim cannot travel and a third-party certificate does not fit a short supply chain.

First-party

A verbal declaration is made by a farmer or producer directly to the consumer, that their produce is produced in alignment with organic principles. There is no verification of the claim; it is a trust-based acknowledgment between the farmer and the consumer, based on direct contact and the relationship.

Second-party — a PGS

Stakeholders in the supply chain — other farmers, consumers and local markets — meet regularly, visit farms and assess the farmer's production methods against the group's chosen organic standard. This is the model Outeniqua PGS uses.

Third-party

An independent certification body assesses the operation. Third-party certification to the SAOSO standard provides alignment to global standards and is suitable for long supply chains nationally, and export.

Descriptions condensed from the South African Organic Certification Landscape (PGS SA), pp. 2–3.
