{% comment %} D79 option E's legend, rendered once per answer wherever a citation carries a reviewed row's context line. It answers the one judgement no test can make (UAT Scenario 8): does a reader understand that the dashed line is context the page supplies, and that the text under the solid rule is what the document prints? Both rules are named, in the reader's terms, because a reader who has not seen this pattern before has no reason to know a dashed rule means "not quoted". {% endcomment %}

The dashed line is context, not a quotation: it names the table row and column the quoted cell sits in, taken from the same printed table. The document does not print those words beside the cell. The text under the solid rule is the document’s own.
